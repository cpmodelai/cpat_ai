3  Methods of the mitigation prototype

The method of each block of the prototype, in the order of the Mitigation sheet, then the distribution tab and the data tabs.

3.1 Prices and energy demand

Source: excel_ai/mitigation/docs/methods/Prices.md.

Equations: Equations.md; workbook formulas: Formulas.md.

How the model projects fuel supply costs and retail prices, following legacy CPAT cut down to the basics, and the existing carbon pricing on top of them (section 5b). Subsidy phase-outs and price liberalisation are left out; electricity prices are in chapter Power. The workbook sheet LegacyDiff lists all differences with legacy. Two data rules differ from legacy by user decision (A10, A11); legacy bug L14 is fixed (A10).

Sources: CPAT documentation, Mitigation chapter 3.2 (github.com/cpmodel/cpat_public); legacy workbook CPAT 1.0pre_456_NoPropData.xlsb, sheet Mitigation (rows 452/455 US deflator and CPI, 456/467/725 inflation indices, 633-695 international prices by source) and sheet Prices_int (regional assumptions); coded/cpat_model/mitigation/prices/ (prices.py, domestic_prices.py, international_prices.py), which ports the legacy formulas line by line.

3.1.1 0. Assumptions: what the numbers mean

# Assumption
A1 Every price, tax and policy value is in real US dollars of the results year (ScenarioDefinition ResultsYear, 2026, from scenario 1), per GJ. A constant real price is a constant number. There is no inflation after the conversion, and fuel use responds only to real price changes.
A2 Two indices, both 1 in the results year. The CPI index (US CPI of the results year / of the year) converts nominal domestic values; the deflator index (US GDP deflator, same form) converts nominal international prices. This is the legacy split (CPI rows 456/725, deflator row 467; row 630 is its reciprocal). Up to 2030 they differ by up to 1.2% (2022: 1.137 vs 1.124; 2030: 0.918 vs 0.929), so one index would not reproduce legacy.
A3 Domestic price data are nominal USD of their own year (IMF price dataset, Prices_dom: supply cost, excise and other taxes, retail price). Each is converted with that year’s CPI index and divided by GJ per price unit. Units: $/liter for gasoline, diesel, LPG and kerosene; $/bbl for other oil products; $/GJ for coal, gas and biomass.
A4 Forecasting coefficients are base-year (2022) nominal values: the margin over international prices and the domestic production cost. They are converted with the 2022 CPI index and held constant in real terms. (coded applies no conversion here; to check against legacy in bucket 5.)
A5 International prices are nominal by source (IMF, WB, IEA, EIA, averages, Manual: IMF to 2025, then sheet Manual inputs D221:AF225 at the legacy addresses, by label and year), converted with the deflator. Only their year-on-year ratio moves the supply cost, so their units and level drop out. Beyond a source’s horizon the data hold them constant in real terms.
A6 ScenarioDefinition policy inputs are real (results-year USD): the carbon price, feebate rates and fuel price reform increases (in price units, e.g. $/liter real). Only the carbon price has a nominal option: NomorReal = Nominal makes the path nominal, then multiplied by the CPI index (legacy row 2249).
A7 History vs projection. Prices up to the last historical price year (Settings C10; default 2022, as legacy) come from data; later years are projected. Fuel use is data in the base year (2022) and modelled from 2023. The IMF dataset also holds 2023-2024; setting C10 to 2024 uses them (issue I89).
A8 Global vs per scenario. Global (Settings, ScenarioDefinition scenario 1): results year, government price controls (pass-through), last historical year, the country’s gas market. Per scenario: international price source and High/Low adjustment, carbon price and its nominal/real switch, fuel price reform, feebates, coverage.
A9 Horizon. Values after 2030 are not compared with legacy. The legacy CPI is held flat after 2031 (WEO horizon), the deflator is not, and some legacy rules (A10) misbehave over long horizons. The model computes to 2041 (the legacy horizon of the power block), but 2031-2041 are not a target.
A10 Legacy rules are copied as they are, except the bugs. v0.4.10 (user rule: bugs are never replicated): legacy bug L14 is fixed: with pass-through 0.5 or 0.8, legacy’s other taxes jumped in the first projected year (the floating subsidy multiplied by (1 - pcc) a second time); the prototype applies (1 - pcc) once (no effect for Egypt, pass-through 0 or 1). Changed (user decision): other oil products are treated like the other oil products, taking pass-through and margin from the dataset (Egypt: 0 and 7.95 $/bbl) instead of the legacy hardcodes 1 and 0. Under the legacy rule their retail price fell with the oil price to the 0.01 floor (3.79 $/GJ in 2024, 0.23 in 2030), and their fuel use rose 10x by 2030.
A11 VAT rate (user decision). Use the dataset’s VAT rate where it is filled. Otherwise use the country’s general VAT rate (Egypt 14%) for residential coal and gas and the all-sector oil products (gasoline, diesel, LPG, kerosene, other oil products), and 0 for power, industry (VAT is credited to firms) and biomass (largely informal). For Egypt the dataset’s own retail prices imply exactly this: rp = (sp + txo) x 1.14 for residential gas and the oil products, and rp = sp + txo for power and industry. So historical oil-product retail prices equal the dataset’s (14% higher than without the rule), and new policies pay 14% VAT in those sectors.

3.1.2 1. Dimensions and units

  • Prices are kept by fuel x price sector, as in the IMF dataset: 12 price fuels, namely coal and natural gas for power, residential and industry; gasoline, diesel, LPG, kerosene, other oil products and biomass for all sectors. Electricity is left to the power module. Subsectors take the price of their price sector (road and residential use the residential coal and gas prices).
  • All in real $/GJ of the results year (A1-A3).

3.1.3 2. International prices gp

gp_t = international price of the source and commodity x deflator x adjustment factor.

  • Source: ScenarioDefinition IntEnerPricForeSource, asterisk dropped (IMF-WB* = average of IMF and WB).
  • Commodity: crude oil (gasoline, diesel, LPG, kerosene, other oil products); coal; gas of the country’s market (Egypt Global = average of LNG, North America and Europe); a flat 1 (biomass).
  • Factor (ScenarioDefinition IntEnerPricForecastAdjustment): Base = 1; High / Low = 1.5 / 0.5 for oil and 1.25 / 0.75 for gas and coal, after the last historical year.

3.1.4 3. Supply cost sp

Data up to the last historical year L; then sp_t = fixsp + (sp_{t-1} - fixsp - ps_{t-1}) x gp_t / gp_{t-1} + ps_t: a fixed part, a part that floats with the international price, and the existing producer subsidy ps (block row psp, $/GJ; legacy sp = fixsp + fltsp + ps, rows 2455-2708; v0.6.1, I49). The producer subsidy phase-out is not in sp: it is a separate new-policy wedge (section 6b; v0.6.2).

fixsp = (margin + bucketed pass-through x production cost [coal, gas]) x CPI index(2022) / GJ per unit. The margin of biomass is 0 (legacy hardcode; other oil products from data, A10). Producer subsidies enter through ps (section 6b; 0 for Egypt).

3.1.5 4. Excise and other taxes txo (pass-through)

  • Pass-through pcc: raw = the dataset’s base-year value (blank = 0; biomass = 1; other oil products from data, A10). Bucketed: <= 0.25 -> 0, <= 0.5 -> 0.5, else 1. Settings GovPriceControls chooses Bucketed (default), Manual or None = 1. Manual takes the retail price control (pass-through) of the block from sheet Manual inputs J167:J180, the legacy addresses, by the code in column G (mit.rp.<fuel>.<sector>.ctrl; legacy default 0.8 for every block); a blank cell gives 1, as legacy rows 747-760. The electricity rows (J178:J179) are not read: electricity prices come from the power section. Egypt bucketed: coal 1, gas 0, oil products incl. other oil products 0, biomass 1.
  • Historical txo (as legacy): coal, gas and biomass rp / (1 + vr) - sp; oil products the dataset’s txo (legacy builds their retail price as sp + txo, times (1 + vr)).
  • Projected: txo_t = txo_L x pcc + cs_t, with the floating subsidy cs_t = (sp_L - sp_t + txo_L(1 - pcc)) x (1 - pcc), and cs_t not below txo_L(1 - pcc) when that is >= 0.
  • pcc = 1: supply-cost changes are passed on in full. pcc = 0: the retail price before new policies stays at its value in L, and the floating subsidy absorbs the change.

3.1.6 5. Retail price before new policies rpb and the after-tax price atp

rpb = (sp + txo) x (1 + vr), with vr the VAT rate of L (A11). For gasoline, diesel, LPG and kerosene vr is the VAT rate x the household share of the product (sheet OilProductShares; Egypt: gasoline 1, diesel 0.03, LPG 0.99, kerosene 0.39), as legacy rows 797-816: VAT is charged on the household part only, firms reclaim it (v0.4.12, LD5; issue I39). Other oil products and the other fuels take the full rate, as legacy.

By subsector: atp = max(rpb + (nce + xcpd) x (1 + vr), 0.01). New policies (nce = new carbon tax + new ETS cost + new excise) are passed through in full and pay VAT (legacy vat = (sp + txo) x vr with nce inside txo); xcpd is the change in existing carbon pricing (section 5b). The 0.01 floor is legacy’s.

Residential LPG and kerosene always exempted (ScenarioDefinition AlwaysExemptResLPGKer, row 268, by scenario; default No; legacy rows 5546-5547). If the switch starts with “Yes” (Yes or Yes), the new carbon tax, the new ETS cost and the new excise of the residential LPG and kerosene rows are 0: each is multiplied by the exemption factor of Section-1 rows exf (exf.all = 1, exf.res = 0 if Yes), at the row’s position in Inputs column exg (2 for residential LPG and kerosene, 1 elsewhere). Their nce is then 0, so atp = max(rpb + (xcpd + csw + pcw) x (1 + vr), 0.01): no new policy and no VAT on it, while existing taxes, the change in existing carbon pricing and the subsidy phase-outs stay, as legacy (row 2651, the price before new policies). The shadow price shp is not exempted, as legacy. The new-policy revenue of these rows (rnew, the excise revenue rex and the VAT on the new policies in rvt) is 0 with the price. ETS-covered emissions still count them where the residential sector is in an ETS. The residential leakage to biomass reads the row’s own nce, so an exempt fuel does not leak (Equations.md 4a).

Layout of a price block and the price incl. new policies by block

Each fuel | sector block of section 2 reads: settings (italic: parameter row, international reference price gpi, fixed supply cost spf, existing subsidy per price unit esubpu); the full retail price rpn (bold); the build-up sp, txf, txo, etx, esub, vat ending with rpb; then the new taxes and subsidies.

New policies act by subsector (sections 5-8). The block shows them as averages over the subsectors priced by the block (the atp rows whose D is the block position), weighted by their fuel use ener:

  • pnq = sum of ener over those subsectors (ktoe);
  • pctx, pets, pntx, pxcd = fuel-use weighted averages of ctxnew, ets, ntx, xcpd;
  • rpn = sum(atp x ener) / pnq (rpb where pnq = 0);
  • pnce = rpn - rpb, the total new policy; pvatn = pnce - (pctx + pets + pntx + pxcd), the VAT on the new policies (and the 0.01 floor).

These are weighted averages with each scenario’s own quantities, not price indices; price changes between scenarios are the indices of section 7.

3.1.7 5b. Existing carbon pricing

Legacy CPAT adds existing carbon taxes and ETS from its sheet ECP (WB Carbon Pricing Dashboard, ICAP; as of 1 April 2025) as parts of txo (Mitigation rows 1675-1764). Since v0.4.12 the prototype follows legacy with several schemes (user decision 2026-10-18; was issue I7), with legacy bugs L61-L65 fixed. It differs in how the level enters: the IMF retail prices already contain whatever existing carbon pricing a country had, so only the change against that level enters (below); legacy subtracts the same level from the fixed part of txo, which gives the same numbers.

  • Data (sheet ECP at its legacy addresses, 52 countries; Egypt has none). Each code is looked up in its own legacy block (sheet Inputs_ecp, columns K:L give the block’s first and last row):
    • carbon tax: single rate ecr.all, fuel rates ecr.<fuel>, sector coverage ecs.<sector>, fuel coverage ecf.<fuel> (rows 69-559);
    • national ETS: price eer.all, coverage ees, eef (rows 600-615, 619-737, 1040-1093);
    • regional EU ETS: the EU price (row 616) and the country’s regional coverage (rows 738-1036, 1094-1223), where the country’s “EU ETS?” flag (general information, column K) is Yes or Both (legacy row 1695).
  • Prices in real USD of ResultsYear (nominal x CPI index). Coverage of each data year (2021-2025; blank = 0, as legacy), the last data year after.
  • Carbon tax rate by fuel (legacy rows 1752-1764): the fuel rate where the country has one and no “single price” flag (general information, column E), else the single rate.
  • Sector of a price: the ECP sector of the price fuel, as legacy: coal and gas by their price sector (power, residential, industry); oil products, biomass and jet fuel transport; other oil products industry (power: power).
  • Tables (sheet Inputs_ecp), by ECP sector | fuel and data year, $/tCO2: carbon tax = rate x sector coverage x fuel coverage; ETS = national price x its coverage + EU price x the regional coverage (the two schemes add up, as legacy).
  • Scenario factors (section 1, ScenarioDefinition): xct.p = 1 if ExistingCTApply = Yes, x (1 + ExistingCTGrowth)^(years after the last data year), else 0; xets.p the same with ExistingETSApply, ExistingETSGrowth (legacy grows the EU ETS with the carbon-tax growth: bug L61). xct.c is the data column of the year.
  • Per subsector and fuel: level xcp = EF x (carbon tax x xct.p + ETS x xets.p) ($/GJ); change xcpd = the level minus the level embedded in the price data (xcp0, Inputs: the tables in the last historical price year L). Only the change enters the after-tax price, so a country’s history is not counted twice: switching a scheme off lowers the price by its embedded level; growth or new data raise it.
  • Power: the same change per kWh (row pow | fuel of the tables) is part of the variable cost before new policies, so dispatch, investment and electricity prices respond.
  • New carbon tax: if CTaxComplimentaryToETS = Yes (legacy rows 2270-2288), it covers 1 - the national ETS sector coverage of the year (legacy: of the previous year, bug L65). Otherwise new instruments add to existing ones.
  • Revenue: existing-tax revenue includes xcpd x (1 + vr); a memo shows the level, fuel use x xcp. Existing ETS revenue is valued at the full permit price, as legacy; legacy’s free-allocation and revenue data (ECP rows 560-597, 1225-1322) are not used in legacy’s model either.
  • Legacy bugs fixed (register): L61 (EU ETS growth), L62 (transport diesel, LPG and kerosene take the rate row as coverage), L63 (fuel coverage lookup stops at row 403 of a block that ends at 425), L64 (no ETS on other oil products outside power), L65 (complementary tax with last year’s coverage).

3.1.8 6. Existing taxes and subsidies

Per price fuel and year, real $/GJ:

Item Formula Use
VAT (sp + txo) x vr VAT before new policies (revenue)
Existing excise and other taxes max(txo, 0) revenue
Existing consumer subsidy min(txo, 0) negative (subsidies are negative)
Subsidy per price unit subsidy x GJ per price unit in ScenarioDefinition price units ($/liter, $/bbl, $/GJ), for a later per-fuel subsidy reform through the fuel price reform inputs, which use the same units

Legacy splits txo into a fixed tax, a fixed subsidy and a floating part; the sign split gives the same totals. VAT on oil products is (sp + txo) x rate x household share; legacy divides the retail price by 1 + the full rate (bug L60). Revenue, as fuel use (PJ) x the rate: existing taxes = excise + VAT + xcpd x (1 + vr); existing subsidies = the subsidy (negative); net existing = taxes + subsidies; new = new carbon tax + new excise + ETS x revenue factor + VAT on new policies. Power adds its rows (chapter Power).

3.1.9 6b. Subsidy and price-control phase-outs, producer subsidies (v0.4.16; LD18, LD19)

User decision 2026-10-18: separate policy lines by sector and fuel, not meshed with the pre-policy price build-up. sp, txo and rpb are unchanged; the reforms are wedges added to the after-tax price, like the new policies.

  • Factors (section 1, legacy rows 2211-2218). One per phase-out: producer subsidies (ScenarioDefinition 123-129), consumer subsidies (131-137) and price controls (191-195). Scenario 1 reads the baseline switch, period and share; other scenarios the policy switch and period, with share = MAX(baseline, policy) (legacy rows 264-274). The price-control phase-out applies if its switch is on and the scenario is a policy scenario or its baseline switch is on, and it takes the consumer share (as legacy: issue I48). Factor pof = MAX(1 - share, 1 - share x (year - start) / period after the start year): the share of the original kept, the same year for every line (legacy lags the fixed subsidy a year: bug L81).
  • Lines by price fuel | sector. csf.<block> = 1 - csi x (1 - factor), pcf.<block> likewise with pci; the inclusion switches csi, pci (sheet Constants, default 1 for every block, as legacy) let a reform cover some fuels and sectors only. Producer subsidies by fuel (coal, gas, oil, biomass, electricity): psi, psf.
  • Split of txo (each price block). Fixed part txfx = pass-through x txo (history: of the year; projection: of the last historical year); floating part txfl = txo - txfx (it absorbs the supply-cost changes not passed through). As legacy rows 2597-2599.
  • Wedges (each block, $/GJ before VAT). Consumer subsidy reform pcsw = -(1 - csf) x MIN(txfx, 0): only a fixed subsidy goes (legacy: the fixed subsidy x factor). Price-control phase-out ppcw = -(1 - pcf) x txfl: the floating part goes, subsidy or tax (legacy row 2599). For Egypt every subsidised fuel has pass-through 0, so only the price-control phase-out removes subsidies, as in legacy.
  • Subsectors. csw, pcw = the wedges of the subsector’s block; atp = MAX(rpb + (nce + xcpd + csw + pcw) x (1 + vr), floor). Revenue rsr = fuel use x PJ/ktoe x (csw + pcw) x (1 + vr): the subsidy saved plus VAT on it (section 12; 12e books it under existing taxes and subsidies, its VAT under VAT). rnet stays the pre-reform existing revenue.
  • Producer subsidies (fiscal line). pros.<fuel> = Prices_dom mit.pros.<fuel>.all (USD bn nominal) x CPI factor, held after the last price year; rpsu.all = -SUMPRODUCT(pros, psf) in section 12 (negative = cost), in the total revenue and the fiscal space; 12e producer subsidies = the electricity subsidy gap + rpsu. Price effect (v0.6.1, I49, as legacy rows 825-829): section-1 rows psd.<fuel> hold the demand that carries the subsidy: oil, world demand x liters per ktoe (legacy _conv_ktoe_liter_oil = 1,108,792); coal and gas, the average of world demand and domestic consumption (EnergyCons, excluding electricity and heat) x GJ per ktoe; biomass, domestic consumption x GJ per ktoe. psu.<fuel> = -pros x USDperM / psd up to the last price year, then held: the existing subsidy. Each price block reads the psu of its fuel by its international price position (G) into psp ($/GJ: x liters per barrel for other oil products, / GJ per price unit), which stays in the supply cost and so in the price before new policies rpb.

Producer subsidy reform (v0.6.2, user rule 2026-10-20: subsidy reforms are always separate from the before-tax build-up). psr.<fuel> = -psu x (1 - psf) (legacy: the base value x the phase-out factor, row 2213). The block wedge ppsw = psr of its fuel ($/GJ as psp) x the block’s pass-through pcc, beside the consumer subsidy and price-control wedges pcsw and ppcw; the subsectors read it as psw into the after-tax price (atp = rpb + (new taxes + xcpd + csw + pcw + psw) x (1 + VAT)), and the VAT on it is in the subsidy-reform revenue rsr. Legacy puts the phase-out into the supply cost, where the price controls absorb (1 - pcc) of it; the wedge x pcc gives the same price except where the excise floor binds. Legacy bugs fixed: L124 (residential gas read the oil value) and L125 (other oil products added a per-liter value to a per-barrel price). 3.2.1 Power engineer model: method

Equations: Equations.md; workbook formulas: Formulas.md.

Scope. The quantity side of the power sector: power demand (legacy table H), dispatch (I), capacity and retirement (K), investment (L), and the cost rows investment needs: shadow price and rebate (D6), levelised variable cost (D9) and levelised investment cost (E1). The price side is in chapter Power. The legacy reference is Mitigation section 4 of CPAT 1.0pre_456_NoPropData.xlsb: rows 2954-4297 for the baseline, rows 9285-10628 for the carbon-tax run.

Inputs by scenario. Every ScenarioDefinition power input is read in the scenario’s own ScenarioDefinition column (section 1, “Power sector settings”: rows 242, 262, 342-415 and 482), as legacy runs each column with its own runs. Sub-table A0 of section 3 applies them to the power data of the country (Inputs): capacity factor, efficiency, WACC and discounted lifetime, fuel per kWh, base-year capacity, new investment permitted and its start year, scale-up and retirement rates, the early-retirement factor, the capex decline scenario, storage, PPA, renewable subsidy, rebate and excise settings. Scenario 1 is the baseline of every scenario, so a technical input (logit constants, WACC, capacity-factor bounds, storage, cost decline) changed in a policy scenario changes that scenario only, and its difference to scenario 1 mixes the input change with the policy; technical inputs are not usually changed across scenarios. Not read (no prototype feature): CostBreakdownYear, ElasticityModel, PowGenCostsOldNew, UseElasticityModelPowerDemandInEngineerModel, SupplyCostDeltaSubsidy, the coal implicit cost (369-371). The final deficit to gas is a global switch (Settings C45, PwDeficitGas), as legacy’s typed cell.

Alignment. The prototype follows legacy formula by formula, except for ten bugs, which are fixed; Legacy_CPAT_Bugs_v<version>.docx lists them with their effects. power_model_ref.py implements the method in Python with one switch per bug; with all switches off it reproduces 208 cached legacy rows of both runs to 1e-13. Planned retirement is available for every generation type; legacy offers it for coal only.

Links with the rest of the model. Power demand follows its own elasticities (H). Sector policies (carbon tax, ETS, feebates, fuel price reform) act on the fuel blocks only, not on power demand; electricity demand by sector is a memo, not part of the fuel totals, so nothing is counted twice. Power CO2 enters the CO2 totals (by fuel and as ETS sector power, so a new ETS covering power sees it, and the baseline CO2 data include it). Generation fuel use of coal, gas, oil and biomass enters total fuel use; nuclear, wind, solar, hydro and other renewables have no fuel. Power revenues enter the revenue totals: fuel taxes and subsidies on generation, the change in existing carbon pricing, electricity VAT and subsidy gap, and new policies with the ETS at the permit price. Existing carbon pricing changes the variable cost before new policies (chapter Prices section 5b). A Settings switch turns the legacy 2023-2024 demand shock on (default off, as for fuels).

Units: generation and capacity in MW / MWy; demand in ktoe; costs in real $/kWh of 2026.

2. Method (with fixes)

H Demand. Four demand groups (transport, residential, industry, services), with electricity elasticities to income, usage, efficiency and autonomous efficiency (eps_Y, eps_U, eps_F, alpha). - Each group’s price is last year’s residential and industrial prices weighted 0.5/0.5 for transport, 1/0 for residential, 0/1 for industry and services. - Each group’s demand multiple combines autonomous efficiency, GDP growth (with the income factor), the price change and an optional shock: (1 / (1 + alpha + extra))^(1 + eps_U) × (1 + GDP growth)^(eps_Y × inc) × (price ratio)^(eps_U + eps_F + eps_U eps_F) × (1 + shock × switch). - Demand of each of the 15 subsectors = last year’s × its group’s multiple (services use the services multiple in every year). - Required generation (MWy) = demand × (1 + net exports + transmission + own use + statistical difference shares), converted from ktoe. In the base year it is actual generation. - Memo: demand by group, and electricity demand by subsector, sector and in total.

A0 Data of the scenario. - Capacity factor = actual (base-year output / installed capacity) when strictly between the CFOverride bounds of the scenario (wind and solar: CFOverrideIfBelowWndSol; others: CFOverrideIfBelowFosOth; upper CFOverrideIfAbove), else the studies value. Efficiency = actual (output / input) unless below MinimumThermalEfficiency (or without input), else the studies value. - WACC: Country* (country WACC + coal premium), Tech (country WACC + technology premium), else MAX(WACCUserGlobal (User), the income-group WACC (Income) or the studies WACC, MinimumWACC). Discounted lifetime = (1 − (1 + WACC)^−N) / (1 − (1 + WACC)^−1); renewables use the average of wind and solar. - New investment: the type’s NewInv setting; “If Present” = Yes when the type has more than 0.1% of base-year capacity; from the NewInv year (hydro from the base year when its generation share is above one half). - Scale-up rates: coal, gas, hydro MaxNonVREAsPCOfTotalGen; wind and solar the country default (CtryDefault), MaxWindSolarScaleup (UserDefined) or the category rate (Low, Medium, High, Vhigh); the others MaxNonNucOreBioAsPCOfTotalGen. Retirement rate 1/N, hydro 0 if HydroDoesntRetire = Yes. - Capex time factors of the decline scenario of RenewableCostDeclineRate (renewables; others medium); where the Power tab has no series for the option (Low and Vhigh for wind and solar, Vhigh for other renewables), the older series of the same option, else medium.

I Dispatch. - Fixed-output types (wind, solar, hydro, other renewables, oil, nuclear, biomass) generate capacity × capacity factor. - Coal and gas cover the rest. Each first gets the minimum it must supply given the other’s maximum (maximum = capacity × MaximumCoalCF for coal, × MaximumGasCF for gas; 0.9 by default); the remainder is shared by a logit EXP(-kDispatch × variable cost / lowest variable cost), capped at the maximum. - PPAs: a share p of coal and gas generation (PPAProportionCoalGas × phase-out factor) runs at its capacity factor, scaled to the need, whatever its cost: generation = logit dispatch × (1 − p) + effective capacity × (coal and gas need / coal and gas effective capacity) × p. The phase-out factor falls linearly to 0 over YearsToPhaseOutPPAs years after YearPPACoalGas when the switch of the scenario (PhaseOutCoalAndGasPPAsBaseline in scenario 1, PhaseOutCoalAndGasPPAsPolicy in the others) is Yes or Yes*; else 1. - Fuel use = generation / efficiency (ktoe); CO2 = fuel use × EF.

K Capacity and retirement. - Retirement = planned retirement (data in nameplate MW per year, if the type’s switch is Yes) or else capacity / lifetime; plus early retirement for coal; at most the capacity. Hydro does not retire. Effective retirement = retirement × capacity factor. - For Egypt the legacy file has no planned-retirement data, so every switch is No. - Early coal retirement: room = MAX(0, maximum wind and solar additions − last year’s increase in generation − last year’s non-coal effective retirements); early = MAX(0, MIN(0.8 × coal effective capacity, room) × (1 − coal share of a logit over coal variable cost and wind and solar investment costs)) / coal capacity factor × factor × (1 − PPA share). The factor is MaxCostBasedEarlyRetirement, and 0 in scenario 1 unless EarlyRetirementInBaseline = Yes (legacy F3993); plants under PPA do not retire early. - Capacity needed = MAX(required generation − (effective capacity − effective retirement), 0). Capacity bought in year t is available in t+1.

L Investment. - First round, permitted types only: logit share EXP(-2 × E1 / lowest permitted E1), limited by a scale-up limit (rate × total effective capacity). - Second round: the remaining deficit goes to types with space left, cheapest first. - Final deficit: with PwDeficitGas = Yes (Settings C45; default No, as legacy’s typed switch E4248) the deficit left after the second round goes to gas (legacy row 4252). - Nameplate investment = effective / capacity factor; USD = nameplate × capital cost of the year. - phi = nameplate / (capacity + nameplate − retirement), the new-vintage share used in the vintage averages B2 and B6.

D5, D6, D9, E1. Each sector has three policy rates ($/tCO2), the same for the fuel blocks and power: - carbon tax = carbon price × sector coverage × (1 − ETS coverage); - ETS cost = ETS tax-equivalent price × ETS coverage × (1 − OBR share); - shadow price = feebate (and regulation) rate × share impacting efficiency + ETS tax-equivalent price × ETS coverage × OBR share.

New policy cost per kWh (D5) = tCO2/kWh × (carbon tax × fuel coverage + ETS cost) + fuel price reform per kWh. The levelised fixed cost (C) includes the renewable subsidy per kWh of the scenario (chapter Power 3.3). The shadow price per kWh is the power shadow price × tCO2/kWh; the rebate returns it in proportion to base-year shares, per kWh generated. Total variable cost = cost before new policies + new policy cost + shadow price + rebate. D9 = discounted average of the total variable cost to 2041, the last year (as legacy). E1 = MAX(levelised fixed cost + D9, 0.01).

3.3b Renewable subsidies (A17, C5)
  • Subsidy per kWh rns(t) = −(base subsidy RenewableSubsidyBL + additional subsidy RenSubsidyAddUSDkwh × MAX(0, 1 − (t − start)/n) from the start year RenSubsidyAddStartYear, policy scenarios only), with n = RenSubsidyAddPhaseoutYrs. The inputs are labelled nominal and used as real, as legacy.

3.4 D. Current variable costs
  • Fuel price for power (A18; v0.6.2, legacy bug L126): average of the fuel price before new policies fpn over t-4 .. t from the base year (one year in 2022, five from 2026); legacy averages t-2 .. t+2, which reads later years. The spot price is a switch. Row fpn holds this year’s price, so the average reads one row and only the scenario’s own years.
  • Fuel cost per kWh = fuel price × 0.0036 / ν. Nuclear: 0.004 $/kWh. Renewables 0.
  • Change in existing carbon pricing against the price data (chapter Prices section 5b): tCO2/kWh × the change in the existing carbon tax and ETS price, weighted by their coverage of power and the fuel (0 for Egypt).
  • Variable cost before new policies = variable O&M + fuel cost + change in existing carbon pricing.
  • Carbon cost per kWh generated = the new policy wedge on the fuel in power ($/GJ) × 0.0036 / ν. The wedge sums the carbon tax (carbon price × EF × fuel coverage × power coverage × (1 − ETS coverage of power)), the ETS (tax-equivalent price × EF × ETS coverage of power × (1 − OBR share of power)) and the fuel price reform for coal and gas in power.
  • Total variable cost = cost before new policies + carbon cost + shadow price + rebate (feebates and the output-based part of the ETS). Dispatch uses it.
3.5 E. Total costs
  • Generation cost before carbon (cost recovery, legacy E3) = amortised fixed cost + variable cost before new policies.
  • Average generation cost = generation-share-weighted average of that cost.
  • Average carbon cost per kWh consumed = share-weighted carbon cost × generation / consumption. The ratio covers losses, own use and net exports, from the base-year balance.

Carbon is kept out of the average generation cost and added separately (legacy, documentation 3.4.1.7), so a rebate can be computed against it.

3.6 F. Memos

F7 switching carbon prices, F1 system cost and F4 carbon intensity are memos. Only F4 (tCO2 per kWh = EF × 0.0036/ν) is built, as a parameter.

3.7 J. Systems integration cost of renewables (storage)
  • VRE share v = share of wind + solar + other renewables (legacy row 3851).
  • Short-term storage (batteries): marginal hours = ast × 18 × v, with ast = 1 the share allocated to VRE and 18 = d(9 v²)/dv. Cost per hour per kWh = (battery capex + interface capex / 2 hours kWh/kW) / (dlf of renewables × 8,760) + battery opex / 8,760.
  • Long-term storage (electrolysers): marginal units = LongTermStorageFor100pcVRE × 2 × max(0, v − x) / (1 − x)², with x = StartingPointWhenLTStorageIsNeeded (0.75). Cost per unit = (electrolyser capex + LT storage capex per kWh × StorageHoursForLTStorage) / (dlf of renewables × 8,760) + fixed opex / 8,760 + variable opex.
  • Marginal storage cost of wind, solar and other renewables = hours × cost per hour + units × cost per unit; 0 for other types.

Legacy has a 33% “allocation of LT storage to VRE” parameter and applies it twice (rows 3911 and 3912, a bug). The prototype applies it once.

3.8 G. End-user prices (residential and industrial)
  • Supply cost (I5, lagged) = last year’s average generation cost + transmission (0.040 $/kWh residential, 0.015 industrial). In the base year, the base-year average cost.
  • Price before new policies (S4): up to the last historical year L, the historical retail price including VAT from the price dataset (nominal → real with the CPI index); after L, last year’s value + pass-through × change in supply cost × (1 + VAT of L), with pass-through 1 (legacy hardcode).
  • End-user price = price before new policies + (average carbon cost × pass-through × (1 − rebate switch) + excise) × (1 + VAT). As in legacy (rows 3614-3617), VAT is charged on the new policy terms.
  • Rebate switch (legacy rows 261, 2188, J3595): 1 in policy scenarios with PowerRebates = Yes (or Yes*) or a feebate scenario of the legacy scenario table (Feebates, Power Supply Feebates, Power feebate); the carbon cost is then not passed to electricity users.
  • Excise per kWh before VAT = electricity excise + pass-through × (manual excise − its value in the last historical year) after that year. The electricity excise (scenario Electricity excise, policy scenarios; legacy row 2253) = grid CO2 intensity of the base year (CO2 of the fuel input to power / power output, Balances) × carbon price. The manual excise (policy scenarios with AdditionalExcise = Yes; legacy rows 3602, 3631) is the new electricity excise of ‘Manual inputs’ rows 161-162 (real, or nominal × the deflator index if NomorReal is not Real); like legacy it enters the price before policy, so up to the last historical year it is absorbed by the price data and later its change is passed through.
  • Subsidy gap (S7) = price before new policies / (1 + VAT) − supply cost. Negative = price below the modelled supply cost (subsidies are negative).
3.9 G4 and M. Revenues (USD million real) and the revenue totals

By user group (G), each × consumption of the group: - VAT = price before new policies × VAT / (1 + VAT); - VAT on the new-policy cost passed through = (average carbon cost × pass-through × (1 − rebate switch) + excise) × VAT; - rebate (negative) = − rebate switch × average carbon cost × pass-through: the carbon cost returned to users (the VAT on it is not collected); - excise = excise per kWh × consumption, of which the electricity excise of the scenario (carbon-based) and the manual excise; - subsidy gap (signed: negative = fiscal cost, positive when the price is above the modelled supply cost); - new-policy cost (memo; it values the ETS at the tax-equivalent price) = average carbon cost.

By generation type (M), with a fuel flag of 1 for coal, gas, oil and biomass: - existing taxes on generation fuels = flag × fuel use × (excise + VAT) of the price fuel; - existing subsidies on generation fuels = flag × fuel use × subsidy (negative); - change in existing carbon pricing × generation; - new policies = generation × carbon cost − CO2 × the power ETS cost rate × (1 − revenue factor): the ETS part at the permit price, as in the subsectors; - existing carbon pricing level (memo) = CO2 × the covered existing carbon tax and ETS prices.

The revenue totals add a power row: existing taxes = fuel taxes + change in existing carbon pricing + electricity VAT; subsidies = fuel subsidies + subsidy gap + renewable subsidies; new policies = new-policy revenue by type + VAT on new policies + rebate + excise; existing carbon pricing level = the memo by type. In the revenue by policy (12e), the rebate and the carbon-based excise count with the carbon tax, the manual excise with the excise, and the renewable subsidies in their own row (as legacy rows 7749, 7762, 7903, the base subsidy once). The fuel subsidy and the subsidy gap do not overlap: the supply cost already uses the subsidised fuel price, so the gap is what remains between that cost and the tariff.

3.3.1 Road transport, engineer model: method

Equations: Equations.md section 4b; workbook layout and names: chapter Transport; bugs fixed and decisions against legacy: chapter Transport; the legacy model: Transport_Legacy_Survey.md and chapter Transport.

1. What it is

The “technoeconomic” road model of legacy CPAT and of the methodology note (data/source/Transportation_methodology.docx). It projects road vehicles, distance driven, fuel economy and energy use from vehicle economics, instead of the single fuel-use equation of the elasticity approach.

  • Vehicle classes: passenger and light vehicles (new and used), freight trucks and 2-3 wheelers; buses follow passenger vehicles.
  • Powertrains: gasoline, diesel, LPG and CNG/LNG; PHEVs (passenger); BEVs.
  • Fuels covered: road gasoline, diesel, LPG, natural gas and electricity; road biofuels follow them as legacy (v0.4.12, LD62): bioethanol and other biofuels with gasoline, biodiesel with diesel (base year x use / base-year use). The vehicle feebate’s CO2 per liter of gasoline and diesel is scaled by their fossil share in the base year (legacy E2146:E2147; issue I40, replicated). Coal, kerosene and other oil products in road, and rail, aviation and navigation, stay on the elasticity approach.

Switch: ScenarioDefinition TrModel (legacy row 340), per scenario. - Elasticity (default): results are exactly those of v0.3.4. - Engineer: section 5b replaces road gasoline, diesel, LPG, gas and biofuels in section 5 in every year. Road electricity feeds power demand from the year after the base year. CO2, revenues, methane, air pollutants and the totals follow.

Section 5b is computed in every scenario either way, so the two approaches can be compared side by side.

2. How it works (one year at a time)

  1. Vehicle demand:
    • Passenger ownership per 1,000 people follows a Gompertz curve in GDP per capita. Its saturation level comes from the data, and it adjusts partially each year, with limits on annual growth and decline.
    • Calibration scales the curve to observed ownership in the last data year, up or down.
    • Freight and 2-3 wheelers: freight ownership is an income-dependent share of passenger ownership; 2-3 wheelers follow a hump in income.
  2. Stock: vehicles retire at 1/lifetime of the trailing-average stock. Purchases are demand minus the remaining stock, split between new and used vehicles by the used share.
  3. Total cost of ownership (TCO) per km, ICEV and BEV:
    • Purchase price after customs, ad-valorem tax, VAT, feebates and EV subsidies.
    • Depreciation and resale value, insurance and maintenance.
    • Fuel or electricity:
      • retail prices incl. carbon tax and fuel reform;
      • EV electricity at last year’s power price plus a charging mark-up that falls with EV penetration.
    • Distance tax.
    • Range anxiety for BEVs: it falls as charging stations grow; charging stations grow with the BEV stock and with charging subsidies.
    • Discounting: at a consumer discount rate that falls with income.
  4. Choice:
    • EV share of purchases: a logit in TCO per km, separately for new and used passenger vehicles, freight and 2-3 wheelers.
    • Fuel split among ICEVs: own- and cross-price elasticities of TCO.
  5. Fuel economy:
    • New vehicles: improves autonomously and with the fuel price (incl. the feebate per liter).
    • Stock: turns over with purchases.
    • Distance per vehicle: responds to the cost per km and to income.
  6. Fuel use: vehicles × distance / fuel economy, by class and fuel, in two calibration steps to the base-year energy balance:
    • distance factors by class;
    • then a residual factor by fuel.
  7. Buses: base-year bus fuel scales with passenger fuel use by fuel; bus electricity scales with passenger electricity.

Policies: all are scenario rows on ScenarioDefinition (rows 418-479), off by default: - carbon tax, fuel price reform and existing taxes (through the retail prices); - feebates by class (rate per t CO2, pivot); - distance tax; - EV purchase subsidy; - ad-valorem purchase taxes; - VAT and customs on BEVs; - charging-station subsidy.

3.4.1 Non-CO2 GHGs and methane: method

Equations: Equations.md; workbook formulas: Formulas.md.

The model adds methane, nitrous oxide and F-gases to energy CO2 and covers every UNFCCC inventory category, so it reports total GHG emissions. It includes a methane fee, its revenue, and combustion methane for every sector.

2. Methane by source

Seven sources: oil and gas extraction, downstream gas, coal mining and biomass use (fossil methane, part of energy); livestock and rice (agriculture); waste.

  1. Allocation. Inventory methane of each category in GHGYear is split between its sources. Fossil sources: weight = MIN(10 x the source’s share of the four activities, the country’s input share), normalized to sum to 1. Livestock and rice take their input shares; waste takes all waste methane.
  2. Activity. Oil and gas: gas and oil production; coal: coal production (both held at the base-year balance). Downstream gas and biomass: total gas and biomass use of the scenario, power included. Livestock, rice, waste: an index (1 in the base year) that grows with income and population.
  3. Abatement. Fee per tonne CH4 = methane fee (USD per t CO2e) x GWP x 1 if the fee applies to the source (below), else 0. Abatement share = MIN(1, MAX(0, a x fee^b)) on the source’s curve. Biomass and downstream gas use the oil-and-gas curve, as legacy.
  4. Emissions = the source’s inventory methane x activity / activity in GHGYear x (1 - abatement). Shares and GHGYear activity are from scenario 1: the calibration is to the observed (baseline) inventory.
  5. Flaring. The flared share of abated oil and gas methane burns to CO2 (44/16 t CO2 per t CH4), reported as energy CO2.
  6. Revenue = fee x the sum over the sources the fee applies to of CH4 x GWP (fossil or non-fossil), USD million, part of total revenue.
  7. Fiscal space (v0.6.2, I47; user 2026-10-20: as close to legacy as possible with no vulnerability to circular references). The GDP feedback counts the fee revenue as legacy (row 8024) through ch4.revf: the same sum over ch4.emf, which equals ch4.em except for downstream gas and biomass use. Those two read the stored scenario-1 activity (sheet BaselineCO2 rows dg, bm, refreshed by the build; check row ch4bs.chk = 0 in scenario 1), so the fiscal space reads no fuel use and no later change to the demand or power blocks can close a loop through it. Fee, abatement and the other sources are the scenario’s own. The reported revenue (rmf, rtot, block 12e) keeps the own activity. (The v0.6.0 loop came from price-block average ranges that ran into section 11, fixed in v0.6.2.)

The methane fee (ScenarioDefinition) runs linearly from MPLevelStart in MPIntro to MPLevelTarget in MPOutro and keeps its slope after the target year (as legacy). It adds the carbon price when CTforMethane = Yes. As legacy (v0.4.12, user decision 2026-10-18; LD46):

  • the fee path applies only in policy scenarios (scenario number above 1, legacy row 2238); the carbon price part has no such condition, as legacy row 2239;
  • a switch per source on sheet Manual inputs J196:J200, the legacy addresses (names MFeeBio, MFeeDG, MFeeFF, MFeeAgr, MFeeWst; shown on Settings rows 47-51): biomass (No), downstream gas, fossil fuel production (oil, gas and coal), agriculture (livestock, rice), waste (all Yes). The switches are legacy inputs outside ScenarioDefinition (NORMS 4: main inputs on ScenarioDefinition, ‘Manual inputs’ the second source).

3. Energy methane and N2O

  • Combustion methane = fuel use x emission factor, by subsector and fuel, and for power by generation type. The factor is looked up by legacy EF code as for the local pollutants (v0.9.0: country, else World Bank region, else world; table Inputs_nc). Legacy 465 has these lookups but leaves the CH4 factors blank, so its combustion CH4 is zero (issue I34). The factor moves linearly from its base-year value to its final value in 2039 (EFEndYear, the legacy column), flat after.

3.4.2 Calibration of energy CO2 to the GHG inventory: method

1. Method

Every CO2 emission factor (EF), by subsector and fuel and by generation type, is multiplied by one factor, EFCalib. Since v0.4.11 this is legacy’s rule (user decision 2026-10-18: go with legacy; issue I32 replicated): legacy Mitigation rows 623, 839 and 976-977.

The switch is Settings C27, Yes by default, as legacy (EFsAdjustmentAnnexI and EFsAdjustmentNonAnnexI are both Yes*). With the shipped data (Egypt): 2021 inventory 211.05 Mt against the 2022 estimate 226.97 Mt, EFCalib = 0.930 (legacy: 0.930).

The uncalibrated scenario-1 CO2 is data written by the build, like the ETS baseline: a live formula would be circular (EFs feed carbon prices, which feed fuel use). Scenario-1 fuel use does not depend on the EFs in value (no new carbon price; existing carbon pricing changes only after the historical years), so the calibrated baseline is the uncalibrated one times the factor. Everything downstream follows: CO2 by subsector, fuel and power type; carbon tax, ETS cost and existing carbon pricing per unit of fuel and their revenue; the ETS baseline; energy CO2 in the GHG totals. Scenario-1 fuel use and prices do not change. In policy scenarios, carbon prices per unit of fuel are 7% lower for Egypt, so fuel use, revenue and the GDP effect change.

2. Price

  • A covered sector counts once the ETS applies.
  • Benchmarks run linearly from the start to the target year, flat afterwards. The output-based rebating (OBR) share of a sector = its group’s benchmark, bounded to 0-1.
  • Volatility adjustment = (1 + carbon tax policy risk x impact) / (1 + ETS volatility x impact). At Medium: (1 + 0.2 x 0.5) / (1 + 0.42 x 0.5) = 1/1.1.
  • Cap = baseline covered emissions x (1 + change), with the change linear from the start to the target year (after it, per the continuation input).
  • Effective semi-elasticity = baseline-weighted average over covered sectors of semi-elasticity x (1 − (1 − OBR effectiveness) x OBR share); OBR effectiveness is a setting (Settings C14, legacy value 0.5).
  • Fast estimate = LN(cap / baseline covered) / (effective semi-elasticity x volatility adjustment) when the cap is below the baseline; else 0.
  • Permit price = the override prices if the override switch is Yes (a blank year takes the carbon price path), else the fast estimate; 0 before the ETS applies.
  • Tax-equivalent price = permit price x volatility adjustment; revenue factor = 1 / volatility adjustment.

3. Effect on fuel use and revenue

The ETS replaces the carbon tax in covered sectors. The tax-equivalent price splits by the OBR share:

  • Price wedge (auctioned part) = tax-equivalent price x EF x coverage x (1 − OBR share). It enters the after-tax fuel price, so usage and efficiency both respond, as for a carbon tax.
  • Shadow price (OBR part) = tax-equivalent price x EF x coverage x OBR share, added to the feebate shadow price. It acts only on the efficiency margin of fuel use, as feebates do.

New-policy revenue includes the price wedge x the revenue factor: the permit price on the auctioned part of covered emissions. The OBR part raises no revenue.

4. Meeting the cap

The fast estimate uses one semi-elasticity, so the full model does not hit the cap exactly. The model reports CO2 by ETS sector, covered emissions, the cap, the gap (covered / cap − 1) and a proposed price p x (LN(cap/baseline) / LN(covered/baseline)) ^ 0.5. To meet the cap, either:

  • paste the proposed prices into the override row (with the override switch on) and repeat; or
  • run the goal seek: the macro SolveETSLive (live scenario; RunAllScenarios runs it for every definition with a new ETS and no override), or python ets_goalseek.py WORKBOOK.xlsx <scenario>, which writes the prices to ets_override_s<scenario>.csv for pasting into the override row. Both iterate with legacy’s damped log-space step (mixing, smoothing, adaptive step) and stop when the worst yearly gap |covered / cap − 1| is below the maximum error, or after the maximum number of iterations.

The goal-seek settings are on sheet Manual inputs at their legacy addresses, as legacy (one value for all scenarios):

5. Limits

  • Power is covered like any other sector: the power model gives its emissions, and the ETS cost enters its generation cost.
  • Benchmarks are relative to current emission intensity, and output is proxied by fuel use. Free allocation therefore moves with emissions, and the OBR share equals the benchmark.
  • The volatility adjustment scales behaviour (the tax-equivalent price), not the permit price. VAT on new policies is levied on the tax-equivalent ETS cost.
  • No banking, borrowing, price floors or ceilings, and no CBAM process emissions.

5. prototype design: benchmarks instead of one auction share

The legacy auctioned proportion is replaced by benchmarks per covered subsector. Everything after the split stays as in section 4.

  • Inputs per subsector: the covered flag and a benchmark path b(t), a share of the subsector’s base-year emission intensity (e.g. 0.9 → free allocation at 90% of base-year intensity; a declining path tightens it).
  • Shares: free allocation FA = b × base-year intensity × output, with output proxied by baseline fuel use (the prototype has no output variable). OBR share s = MIN(FA / covered emissions, 1); auctioned share a = 1 − s. To first order s ≈ b (intensity unchanged). Using actual covered emissions would be circular through fuel use, so the recommendation is scenario-1 (baseline) covered emissions, as in option A of ETS_Cap_Design_Options_v6_4.md; s is then a data-driven input.
  • Then as legacy: effectiveness e = a + 0.5 × s per subsector; the auctioned part p × a is a price wedge (revenue = p × (covered emissions − FA)); the OBR part p × s joins the sector shadow price (the feebate channel); the fast estimate p = (reduction left for the ETS) / (ε × e), with ε and e weighted by covered baseline emissions.
  • Override: an offline goal seek (a Python script with the same damped log-space step) writes a row of prices to paste. No VBA and no circular reference.

Without benchmarks the ETS is fully effective (permit price = carbon price path × coverage, as effective as a carbon tax, per an earlier user decision), and the auction share only affects revenue. With benchmarks, the OBR part moves from the price wedge to the shadow price, so emissions fall less for the same p and revenue falls. A benchmark of 0 (full auctioning) reproduces the fully effective case exactly.

6. Open points

  1. The fast estimate uses legacy’s sector semi-elasticities. The prototype could derive them from its own elasticities (one-off calibration run) to keep them consistent.
  2. Legacy’s linear split (p × a) vs a log form (legacy’s own TBC note).
  3. Legacy’s relative price 1.1 (auctioned ETS vs tax) and the risk-premium inputs (rows 1811–1813) are not used by the fast estimate as cached. Check their role before carrying them over.
  4. The CBAM sector semi-elasticity (−0.001) is not modelled in the prototype.

3.6.1 GDP effects: method

Equations: Equations.md; workbook formulas: Formulas.md. Decisions: Old/GDP_Plan_v3_6.md.

The model estimates how spending the revenue gained by the policies changes GDP. The change feeds back into GDP growth, which drives fuel use, power demand and the non-energy GHG drivers. The method follows legacy CPAT section 17, with legacy bug L24 fixed.

1. Inputs (Egypt defaults)

  • Revenue use, % of revenue (ScenarioDefinition): labour tax cuts 30, corporate tax cuts 0, public investment 50, current spending 0, targeted transfers 20.
  • Multiplier source (ScenarioDefinition row 226): Global, income group, Estimated or Manual; Egypt: income group (LMIC). Range Base/Low/High (row 263): Base. Start year for GDP calculations (row 236, GDPStartYear): 2025. Endogenous GDP effects: Yes.
  • Fiscal multipliers by group, instrument and lag 0-19 (sheet Multipliers, from the legacy sheet: MFMod medians). Low and high are the median minus and plus one standard deviation. Lags 12-19 hold the lag-11 value, as in legacy. v0.4.15 (LD56): “Estimated” takes the country’s table of the legacy Multipliers tab (rows 210-1620, exact match of the country code; the income group where the country is missing, and for CIT and WAGE, whose legacy tables are empty or wrong: bug L76); “Manual” takes sheet Manual inputs J261:U268 (legacy addresses, lags 0-11, blank = 0). Low and high add -/+ the income group’s standard deviation to these too, as legacy (issue I44). Any other source text means the income group.
  • Baseline real GDP (sheet WEO): 547 USD bn in 2022.

2. Method

  1. Fiscal space (v0.4.15, LD55): total revenue of the scenario (existing taxes net of subsidies plus new policies, power included: rnet + rnew, v0.4.16 + subsidy reform rsr and producer subsidies rpsu, as legacy’s rev.new.usd = row 8026, the total of all instruments) minus that of scenario 1, as a share of GDP. Lower fuel use also lowers the cost of existing subsidies (base erosion), and that counts. Scenario 1 has no GDP effect.
  2. Impulse (v0.4.15, as legacy row 14356): the change of that revenue gain in USD over this year’s GDP, 0 before GDPStartYear (issues I41, I42). Each impulse starts a cohort that acts for 20 years (lags 0-19), lag 0 in the impulse year (legacy applies lag 1 there: bug L68).
  3. Effect of each use: its share x the sum of impulse x multiplier over the lags. The revenue itself counts as an energy-tax increase (instrument exc, share -1). The uses map to labour tax (pit), corporate tax (cit), public investment (capt), current spending (gnfs) and transfers (trns).
  4. GDP effect: the sum over uses (0 if endogenous effects are off): the change of the GDP level against the baseline, as a share of GDP.
  5. Feedback: the GDP level is 1 + last year’s GDP effect, and growth follows from the baseline growth and the change in that level. The one-year lag avoids a circular reference (revenue depends on fuel use, which depends on growth). Legacy instead adds the effect to the growth rate every year, so a steady effect compounds (L24).

2b. Timing rule (stated v0.6.2, issue I72; user 2026-10-20)

Revenue of year t changes GDP only from year t+1. gdp.lvl(t+1) = 1 + gdp.eff(t), and GDP growth after the feedback reads that level (as legacy row 8776, which reads the previous column). Three consequences, kept as rules:

  1. The fiscal space of year t reads revenue of year t only (no later year, no whole-row lookup).
  2. A same-year GDP effect never reaches same-year demand: the impulse of year t counts at lag 0 in gdp.eff(t), which acts on GDP from t+1.
  3. Every other block must not read a later year (NORMS, circular references). With this lag, no revenue line can then close a circular reference, whatever it reads (fuel use, power, methane).

The quick check tests the reads of later years; a change that breaks the lag must restate this rule and pass the full recalculation (Err:522).

3. Macros

Core buttons (green, on ScenarioCompare): Recalculate now, Recalculation on/off, Run stored scenarios, Update data tables. The others are grey, below them.

  • RunAllScenarios (button Run stored scenarios): stores the baseline under ID 1 if none is stored yet. Then, for every definition with Run? = Yes, it copies the definition’s inputs and name into the live scenario (skipping two formula rows), clears the ETS price override and recalculates, runs the ETS goal seek if the definition applies a new ETS without “Override ETS price” = Yes, and stores the live scenario’s outputs as values under the definition’s number and name (replacing that ID’s rows wherever they are). At the end it restores the live scenario, the override and the calculation mode it found, sorts StoredOutputs, groups the blank scenario columns and refreshes the dashboard charts.
  • RecalculateNow: full recalculation (timed), then the dashboard charts. The workbook ships with its values in manual calculation; ToggleRecalculation switches automatic calculation on or off (for every open workbook, as Excel does).
  • StoreBaseline: replaces the stored baseline with the current scenario-1 results, after a confirmation.
  • StoreLiveScenario: stores the live scenario as it stands, under an ID you choose (default: the next free ID from 100).
  • SolveETSLive: ETS goal seek for the live scenario only. It fills the override prices and switches the override on.
  • ClearStoredScenarios: deletes every stored scenario except the baseline.
  • SortStoredOutputs: sorts StoredOutputs by code stem, then ID.
  • GroupBlankScenarios: groups and collapses the scenario columns of ScenarioDefinition that have no name.
  • RefreshDashboard: rebuilds the series of the dashboard charts from the scenarios with results in the chosen source (short names in the legend, baseline dashed grey, one colour per scenario slot) and hides the table rows of the others.
  • CheckBatchRun: the first-run test (section 4). It reruns the batch, so the stored scenarios are recalculated by the application that runs it.

The ETS goal seek is legacy’s damped log-space iteration, as in ets_goalseek.py. It starts from the override prices, or from the fast estimate if there are none. Each step is p × (proposed price / p) ^ alpha, mixed 0.3 with the previous iterate, with 5% smoothing. Alpha halves when the error grows (minimum 0.0625) and rises ×1.2 when the error more than halves (maximum 1). It stops when the worst |covered / cap − 1| is below 0.5%, or after 20 iterations.

4. First run in Excel

The VBA project (written by vba_project.py) and the stored results were produced without Excel. They are tested in LibreOffice: the module loads, the macros reproduce the Python emulation, and CheckBatchRun passes. To confirm the same in Excel:

  1. If the file was downloaded, unblock it first (file Properties > Unblock), or Excel opens it with macros disabled.
  2. Open it and choose Enable Content.
  3. Run CheckBatchRun (Alt+F8); it takes a minute or two. It checks the 12 lookups the macros rely on (sheets, rows by code, scenario columns, MTOutputs blocks); compares Excel’s own recalculation of the baseline with the stored baseline (plain formulas only, so Excel and LibreOffice calculate it the same way); and reruns RunAllScenarios, ETS goal seek included, comparing every stored value with the value stored before.
  4. MacroCheck shows Result PASS when no value differs by more than 1e-6 (relative). CHECK lists how many values differ; FAIL names the lookup that was not found.
  5. If Excel cannot load the VBA project, or Alt+F8 lists no macros: remove the project if one is listed, import CPATScenarios.bas (Alt+F11 > File > Import), save as .xlsm and run CheckBatchRun again.

5. Comparing and keeping results

  • Source and year. On ScenarioCompare choose Results from (Stored or Live) and the year, then Recalculate now. Stored reads StoredOutputs (every stored scenario); Live reads MTOutputs (the scenario groups of Mitigation: the baseline and the live scenario, and any group added by copy-paste). The names SCVal, SCCode and SCYear choose the range with CHOOSE; every value is INDEX(SCVal, MATCH(country.stem.ID, SCCode, 0), year column), so the order of the rows never matters.
  • Scenario slots. Up to 10, in ScenarioDefinition order: ID, short name (the legend), name. A scenario with no result in the chosen source shows blank (key table) or #N/A (time series, hidden by a conditional format, a gap in the charts); the charts leave it out.
  • Key table. Energy CO2 emissions, GHG emissions excluding LULUCF, revenue as a share of GDP, the GDP effect of the revenue use and the net welfare effect: level, difference from the baseline, and % difference for the two emission levels. The bar chart beside it shows the % difference of energy CO2 by scenario.
  • Time series. Four tables (default: energy CO2, GHGs, revenue, GDP effect), scenarios down and years across, each with its line chart beside it. The title of each table is a dropdown of the indicator list at the bottom of the tab: every MTOutputs indicator except the price and Kaya indices.
  • StoredOutputs holds values only, so results from other files can be pasted in as values under new IDs, as long as its column layout is kept (output code in column D).
  • The baseline changes only when StoreBaseline is run.

6. Limits

  • The batch run calculates one policy scenario at a time. Further Mitigation scenario groups (copies of group 2) show on the dashboard under Live; the batch macros use the live scenario (group 2) only.
  • MTOutputs (the legacy MTOutputs rows the prototype has and its own key outputs) defines what is stored: its Mitigation rows, each code once (about 830 per scenario); the distribution rows are live only. To store more, add rows to the MTOutputs blocks first.
