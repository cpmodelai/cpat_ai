2  The mitigation prototype

CPAT-AI overview: the mitigation prototype
Status, user guide, results · PowerPoint, latest version

The method of every block, with its equations and pseudocode, is in the CPAT-AI Model tab (open it), shared by Excel-AI and CPAT Python. This chapter is the workbook: how to open and use it, its sections, many scenarios, and its data tabs.

Source: excel_ai/mitigation/docs/Documentation.md.

The short guide. Details of every block: the Methods volume. Every legacy bug and issue: the bug-fix register.

2.1 What it is

The mitigation module of the IMF/World Bank Climate Policy Assessment Tool (CPAT), rebuilt as one readable Excel workbook for one country at a time.

It answers: what do carbon pricing and related policies do to energy prices, energy use, emissions, government revenue and GDP, year by year to 2041?

  • Policies in: carbon tax, a new emissions trading system (ETS) with a cap, feebates, fuel excises, fuel subsidy reform, a methane fee, existing carbon pricing, power-sector policies.
  • Results out: retail energy prices, fuel use in 16 sectors and 8 fuels, electricity generation and prices, revenue, CO₂ and other greenhouse gases, local air pollutants, GDP effects, the gap to the country’s NDC, and household effects by decile (Distribution tab).
  • One country, any country: formulas contain no country numbers. Every country value comes from the data tabs, looked up by the country code on Settings (C4). The shipped data are complete for Egypt.
  • Same as legacy, minus its bugs: it follows legacy CPAT except where legacy has a bug (all fixed) or an open question that we track (issues). See section 5.
  • Readable and checked: every formula is at most 100 characters and one formula serves a whole block of cells. An independent Python recomputation checks every number.

2.2 Quick start

  1. Open CPAT-AI-Mitigation-Prototype_v<version>.xlsm (in excel_ai/; the same file as excel_ai/active_workbooks//CPAT-AI-Mitigation-Prototype_latest.xlsm) in Excel 365 with macros enabled. It opens on ScenarioCompare, the dashboard, without recalculating: the file ships with its values, in manual calculation.
  2. After any change, click Recalculate now (or switch automatic calculation on with Recalculation on/off).
  3. Read the results on ScenarioCompare. Choose Live or Stored results and the year. The key table shows CO₂, GHGs, revenue, the GDP effect and welfare against the baseline; the charts show them over time.
  4. Change a policy on ScenarioDefinition. Column K is the live scenario: for example change CPLevelTarget (carbon price in the target year), then Recalculate now.
  5. Store several scenarios: copy a definition one column to the right, give it a short name (row 2) and Run? = Yes (row 4), then click Run stored scenarios. Results go to StoredOutputs; the dashboard compares up to 10.
  6. Update the data from the data workbook: choose the source on ScenarioCompare, click Update data tables. The status is on Cover.
  7. First time only: click First-run test (grey buttons). The check table must show PASS.

The tabs, left to right: the front (Cover, ScenarioCompare, ScenarioDefinition), the model (Mitigation, Distribution, MTOutputs, StoredOutputs), the inputs (Manual inputs, Settings, Inputs), the data tabs (legacy tables, unchanged), and the reference tabs (version log, differences with legacy, variable dictionary).

2.3 How it works

The Mitigation tab runs top to bottom, one section per step, with the section numbers of legacy CPAT. The method of each block, with its equations and pseudocode, is in the website’s CPAT-AI Model tab (https://cpmodelai.github.io/cpat_ai/#model; sources docs/CPAT_model_documentation_qmd/), shared with CPAT Python; the Word Methods volume (docs/methods/) is the compact version. Each scenario is a group of columns: group 1 is the baseline, group 2 the live scenario. Years run across, 2022 to 2041.

2.3.1 Policies (section 1)

Each policy is a path from a start year to a target year, set per scenario on ScenarioDefinition. The carbon price follows that path:

τ(t) = path(t; start year, target year, start level, target level) · (CPI index if the inputs are nominal)

A new ETS starts at a price estimate and is checked against its cap (the goal seek refines it). Feebates act through a shadow price, which changes efficiency but raises no revenue. Existing carbon taxes and ETS prices come from the World Bank Carbon Pricing Dashboard data.

Details: Methods, chapter Prices; chapter ETS.

2.3.2 Energy prices (section 2)

For each fuel and sector, the retail price builds up from the international price:

supply cost  C(t) = fixed part + (C(t−1) − fixed part) · international price(t) / international price(t−1)
retail price R    = (C + excise and other taxes) · (1 + VAT)
after policy P    = R + new carbon tax + ETS price + new excise, each with VAT

Subsidies are negative taxes. Historical prices come from the IMF price data up to the last historical year (2022, as legacy); later years are projected.

Details: Methods, chapter Prices.

2.3.3 Energy demand (sections 5-8, 11)

Fuel use in each sector changes each year with income, its own price and efficiency:

Q(t) = Q(t−1) · (efficiency gain) · (1 + GDP growth)^(income elasticity) · (P(t)/P(t−1))^(price elasticity) · (efficiency response to prices)

The elasticities are legacy CPAT’s. Section 11 adds up fuel use by sector and fuel, including the fuel burnt in power plants.

Details: Methods, appendix A (equations, section 4); chapter Transport for road.

2.3.4 Power (section 3)

Electricity demand comes from the sectors. The power model, as legacy’s engineer model, decides each year:

  • Dispatch: which plants run, by their variable cost (fuel, carbon price, operation).
  • Investment: new capacity by technology, chosen by levelised cost with a logit share, within scale-up limits.
  • Prices: electricity prices from the cost of generation, with taxes, subsidies and price controls.

Power-sector policies (no new coal, nuclear from a year, renewable subsidies, PPAs, rebating revenue, an electricity excise) are set per scenario.

Details: Methods, chapter Power.

2.3.5 Road transport (sections 5, 5b)

By default road fuel use follows the demand elasticities, as every other sector. With TrModel = Engineer, the road engineer model replaces it: vehicle stocks by class, cost of ownership, the choice between combustion and electric vehicles, and vehicle policies (feebates, EV subsidies, purchase taxes, distance tax).

Details: Methods, chapter Transport.

2.3.6 Revenues (section 12)

Revenue is fuel use times the tax per unit, by fuel and sector, for existing taxes, existing subsidies (negative) and new policies:

revenue from new policies = fuel use · (carbon tax + excise + auctioned share · ETS price + VAT on them)
total revenue             = existing taxes + subsidies + new policies + methane fee

The power sector adds its own revenue and subsidies. Feebates are revenue neutral.

Details: Methods, chapter Macro and results.

2.3.7 Emissions (sections 13-15)

CO₂ = fuel use · emission factor        (factors optionally calibrated to the GHG inventory)

Other greenhouse gases (methane, nitrous oxide, F-gases) follow their drivers; methane from fuel supply responds to the methane fee. Local air pollutants (fine particles, sulphur and nitrogen oxides, and others) are fuel use times pollutant factors.

Details: Methods, chapter Emissions.

2.3.8 GDP, carbon pricing indicators, welfare (sections 16-18)

New revenue is fiscal space. How it is used (tax cuts, investment, transfers) moves GDP through fiscal multipliers, one year later:

fiscal space fs(t)  = (new revenue in the scenario − in the baseline) / GDP
GDP effect          = Σ over uses: share of the use · Σ over lags (change in fs · multiplier of the use)

Section 17 reports carbon prices and coverage; section 18 the welfare effects and the gap to the NDC.

Details: Methods, chapter Macro and results.

2.3.9 Distribution (tab Distribution)

Household effects by decile in one analysis year: price changes reach households directly (energy) and indirectly (other goods, through the input-output table); revenue recycling (transfers, targeted by ASPIRE or the poverty line) offsets them.

Details: Methods, chapter Distribution.

2.4 Results and the dashboard

ScenarioCompare is the place to read results:

  • Choices: Live (the scenario groups of Mitigation) or Stored (every stored scenario), and the year.
  • Key table: energy CO₂, GHGs, revenue, GDP effect and welfare, as levels and differences from the baseline.
  • Charts: one bar chart of CO₂ and four time-series charts; the title of each is a dropdown of indicators.

MTOutputs lists the key indicators of each live scenario by output code; StoredOutputs keeps the stored scenarios as values. Every row carries a code (for example egy.mit.co2.tot.tot.2: country, module, variable, sector, fuel, scenario), the same codes as the Python model.

2.5 Limits and differences from legacy

The rule: never replicate a legacy bug; always replicate a legacy issue, and track it. A bug (L) is legacy computing what it does not intend; the prototype fixes it. An issue (I) is legacy doing what it means to, where the choice is open to question; the prototype does the same. Problems of the input data are issues too (area Data): where legacy reads the data wrongly, the formula fix is a bug with a cell plan and the issue is Very high; where no cell plan is possible, the prototype does exactly what legacy does and the issue is Extremely high (Appendix B). Every item has a code; the codes are explained in Appendix A.

  • Legacy bugs: the prototype fixes every one, in every module (mitigation, distribution, the inputs). The bugs, with the cell-level fix for legacy CPAT and the status of each in the latest legacy file CPAT 1.0pre_465, are in excel_ai/bugs_and_legacy/Legacy_CPAT_Bugs_v<version>.docx; the master list of bugs and issues is the tracker excel_ai/bugs_and_legacy/Tracking_Legacy_Bugs_v<version>.xlsx.
  • Issues (data problems first), departures, prototype bugs and upgrades: the appendices below (and the tracker, tab Issues).
  • Legacy row numbers in the Methods volume are rows of CPAT 1.0pre_456_NoPropData.xlsb, the file the prototype was built and checked against (its data extracts read that file). In CPAT 1.0pre_465 Mitigation rows from 5667 on sit 11 to 15 rows higher. The input sheet ScenarioDefinition has the rows of the 465 MTInputs.
  • Not built: a few legacy features (upgrades, Appendix E), and the air-pollution co-benefits and transport externalities modules.
  • Data: the shipped data are Egypt’s; proprietary tables for other countries must be pasted in or linked (issue I90).

2.6 Running many scenarios

Source: excel_ai/mitigation/docs/methods/Macro_and_results.md.

Equations: Equations.md; workbook formulas: Formulas.md.

The model calculates two scenarios at a time: the baseline and one live policy scenario. A batch macro copies each scenario definition into the live scenario, calculates it and stores its results as values, which are compared afterwards. This follows legacy CPAT, where a macro copies each scenario column into the column used for calculation and stores the outputs.

2.6.3 Main scenario choice and regulations as shadow prices (v0.4.16, LD9)

ScenarioDefinition row 13 (ScenarioSelected, ” - Carbon tax” in every shipped scenario) picks a row of the legacy scenario table (legacy Mitigation rows 222-262, columns N:AC; sheet Mapping, block “Main scenarios”): whether the scenario prices carbon explicitly (legacy row 259), its efficiency adjustment (ScenarioDefinition rows 247-251) and the sectors its shadow price covers (rows 240-257).

  • Explicit scenarios (carbon tax, total carbon price, coal excise, road fuel tax, electricity emissions tax and excise): the carbon price path of ScenarioDefinition rows 18-21 is the carbon tax (cpx = cptraj), as before.
  • Shadow-pricing scenarios (feebates, power supply feebates, energy efficiency regulations, power feebate, vehicle fuel economy, residential and industrial efficiency regulations; and “Baseline”): no explicit carbon tax (cpx = 0); the carbon price path becomes a shadow price on the efficiency margin, reg.<sector> = cp.shd x the scenario’s adjustment (energy efficiency 247, vehicle fuel economy 248, residential 249, industrial 250, feebates 251; Egypt 0.7, feebates 1) x 1 if the scenario covers the sector. It enters shpx (with the feebate part, now x row 251, and the ETS benchmark part), so fuel use responds through efficiency only, and power through shk (legacy bugs L87, L88 fixed: legacy’s regulation share is 0 and never reaches power).
  • The road vehicle feebate of the engineer road model adds reg.rod (the road regulation shadow price) instead of the carbon price, which is in the fuel price already (prototype bug M13).
  • A text that is not in the table counts as explicit pricing (as the shipped ” - Carbon tax”).

2.6.4 Macros

Core buttons (green, on ScenarioCompare): Recalculate now, Recalculation on/off, Run stored scenarios, Update data tables. The others are grey, below them.

  • RunAllScenarios (button Run stored scenarios): stores the baseline under ID 1 if none is stored yet. Then, for every definition with Run? = Yes, it copies the definition’s inputs and name into the live scenario (skipping two formula rows), clears the ETS price override and recalculates, runs the ETS goal seek if the definition applies a new ETS without “Override ETS price” = Yes, and stores the live scenario’s outputs as values under the definition’s number and name (replacing that ID’s rows wherever they are). At the end it restores the live scenario, the override and the calculation mode it found, sorts StoredOutputs, groups the blank scenario columns and refreshes the dashboard charts.
  • RecalculateNow: full recalculation (timed), then the dashboard charts. The workbook ships with its values in manual calculation; ToggleRecalculation switches automatic calculation on or off (for every open workbook, as Excel does).
  • StoreBaseline: replaces the stored baseline with the current scenario-1 results, after a confirmation.
  • StoreLiveScenario: stores the live scenario as it stands, under an ID you choose (default: the next free ID from 100).
  • SolveETSLive: ETS goal seek for the live scenario only. It fills the override prices and switches the override on.
  • ClearStoredScenarios: deletes every stored scenario except the baseline.
  • SortStoredOutputs: sorts StoredOutputs by code stem, then ID.
  • GroupBlankScenarios: groups and collapses the scenario columns of ScenarioDefinition that have no name.
  • RefreshDashboard: rebuilds the series of the dashboard charts from the scenarios with results in the chosen source (short names in the legend, baseline dashed grey, one colour per scenario slot) and hides the table rows of the others.
  • CheckBatchRun: the first-run test (section 4). It reruns the batch, so the stored scenarios are recalculated by the application that runs it.

The ETS goal seek is legacy’s damped log-space iteration, as in ets_goalseek.py. It starts from the override prices, or from the fast estimate if there are none. Each step is p × (proposed price / p) ^ alpha, mixed 0.3 with the previous iterate, with 5% smoothing. Alpha halves when the error grows (minimum 0.0625) and rises ×1.2 when the error more than halves (maximum 1). It stops when the worst |covered / cap − 1| is below 0.5%, or after 20 iterations.

2.6.5 First run in Excel

The VBA project (written by vba_project.py) and the stored results were produced without Excel. They are tested in LibreOffice: the module loads, the macros reproduce the Python emulation, and CheckBatchRun passes. To confirm the same in Excel:

  1. If the file was downloaded, unblock it first (file Properties > Unblock), or Excel opens it with macros disabled.
  2. Open it and choose Enable Content.
  3. Run CheckBatchRun (Alt+F8); it takes a minute or two. It checks the 12 lookups the macros rely on (sheets, rows by code, scenario columns, MTOutputs blocks); compares Excel’s own recalculation of the baseline with the stored baseline (plain formulas only, so Excel and LibreOffice calculate it the same way); and reruns RunAllScenarios, ETS goal seek included, comparing every stored value with the value stored before.
  4. MacroCheck shows Result PASS when no value differs by more than 1e-6 (relative). CHECK lists how many values differ; FAIL names the lookup that was not found.
  5. If Excel cannot load the VBA project, or Alt+F8 lists no macros: remove the project if one is listed, import CPATScenarios.bas (Alt+F11 > File > Import), save as .xlsm and run CheckBatchRun again.

2.6.6 Comparing and keeping results

  • Source and year. On ScenarioCompare choose Results from (Stored or Live) and the year, then Recalculate now. Stored reads StoredOutputs (every stored scenario); Live reads MTOutputs (the scenario groups of Mitigation: the baseline and the live scenario, and any group added by copy-paste). The names SCVal, SCCode and SCYear choose the range with CHOOSE; every value is INDEX(SCVal, MATCH(country.stem.ID, SCCode, 0), year column), so the order of the rows never matters.
  • Scenario slots. Up to 10, in ScenarioDefinition order: ID, short name (the legend), name. A scenario with no result in the chosen source shows blank (key table) or #N/A (time series, hidden by a conditional format, a gap in the charts); the charts leave it out.
  • Key table. Energy CO2 emissions, GHG emissions excluding LULUCF, revenue as a share of GDP, the GDP effect of the revenue use and the net welfare effect: level, difference from the baseline, and % difference for the two emission levels. The bar chart beside it shows the % difference of energy CO2 by scenario.
  • Time series. Four tables (default: energy CO2, GHGs, revenue, GDP effect), scenarios down and years across, each with its line chart beside it. The title of each table is a dropdown of the indicator list at the bottom of the tab: every MTOutputs indicator except the price and Kaya indices.
  • StoredOutputs holds values only, so results from other files can be pasted in as values under new IDs, as long as its column layout is kept (output code in column D).
  • The baseline changes only when StoreBaseline is run.

2.6.7 Limits

  • The batch run calculates one policy scenario at a time. Further Mitigation scenario groups (copies of group 2) show on the dashboard under Live; the batch macros use the live scenario (group 2) only.
  • MTOutputs (the legacy MTOutputs rows the prototype has and its own key outputs) defines what is stored: its Mitigation rows, each code once (about 830 per scenario); the distribution rows are live only. To store more, add rows to the MTOutputs blocks first.
