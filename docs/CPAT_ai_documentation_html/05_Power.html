Power engineer model: method

Equations: Equations.md; workbook formulas: Formulas.md.

Scope. The quantity side of the power sector: power demand (legacy table H), dispatch (I), capacity and retirement (K), investment (L), and the cost rows investment needs: shadow price and rebate (D6), levelised variable cost (D9) and levelised investment cost (E1). The price side is in chapter Power. The legacy reference is Mitigation section 4 of CPAT 1.0pre_456_NoPropData.xlsb: rows 2954-4297 for the baseline, rows 9285-10628 for the carbon-tax run.

Inputs by scenario. Every ScenarioDefinition power input is read in the scenario’s own ScenarioDefinition column (section 1, “Power sector settings”: rows 242, 262, 342-415 and 482), as legacy runs each column with its own runs. Sub-table A0 of section 3 applies them to the power data of the country (Inputs): capacity factor, efficiency, WACC and discounted lifetime, fuel per kWh, base-year capacity, new investment permitted and its start year, scale-up and retirement rates, the early-retirement factor, the capex decline scenario, storage, PPA, renewable subsidy, rebate and excise settings. Scenario 1 is the baseline of every scenario, so a technical input (logit constants, WACC, capacity-factor bounds, storage, cost decline) changed in a policy scenario changes that scenario only, and its difference to scenario 1 mixes the input change with the policy; technical inputs are not usually changed across scenarios. Not read (no prototype feature): CostBreakdownYear, ElasticityModel, PowGenCostsOldNew, UseElasticityModelPowerDemandInEngineerModel, SupplyCostDeltaSubsidy, the coal implicit cost (369-371). The final deficit to gas is a global switch (Settings C45, PwDeficitGas), as legacy’s typed cell.

Alignment. The prototype follows legacy formula by formula, except for ten bugs, which are fixed; Legacy_CPAT_Bugs_v<version>.docx lists them with their effects. power_model_ref.py implements the method in Python with one switch per bug; with all switches off it reproduces 208 cached legacy rows of both runs to 1e-13. Planned retirement is available for every generation type; legacy offers it for coal only.

Links with the rest of the model. Power demand follows its own elasticities (H). Sector policies (carbon tax, ETS, feebates, fuel price reform) act on the fuel blocks only, not on power demand; electricity demand by sector is a memo, not part of the fuel totals, so nothing is counted twice. Power CO2 enters the CO2 totals (by fuel and as ETS sector power, so a new ETS covering power sees it, and the baseline CO2 data include it). Generation fuel use of coal, gas, oil and biomass enters total fuel use; nuclear, wind, solar, hydro and other renewables have no fuel. Power revenues enter the revenue totals: fuel taxes and subsidies on generation, the change in existing carbon pricing, electricity VAT and subsidy gap, and new policies with the ETS at the permit price. Existing carbon pricing changes the variable cost before new policies (chapter Prices section 5b). A Settings switch turns the legacy 2023-2024 demand shock on (default off, as for fuels).

Units: generation and capacity in MW / MWy; demand in ktoe; costs in real $/kWh of 2026.

1. Order within a year and across years

year t:  H  demand(t)        <- demand(t-1), GDP(t), price(t-1)              (no circularity: price lagged)
         K1 capacity(t)      <- capacity(t-1) - retirement(t-1) + investment(t-1)
         I  dispatch(t)      <- capacity(t), required generation(t), variable costs(t)
            shares(t)        -> J VRE share -> storage cost(t) -> B (vintages), C (levelised)
            shares(t)        -> E average generation cost(t) -> G supply cost(t+1) -> price(t+1) -> demand(t+2)
         K  retirement(t)    <- capacity(t), early coal retirement (coal variable cost vs wind and solar costs)
         L  investment(t)    <- required generation(t) - capacity after retirement(t); logit on E1(t)
                             -> capacity(t+1); share in the stock phi(t) -> B vintages(t+1)

The levelised variable cost D9(t) averages the variable cost from t to the end of the horizon. Variable costs depend only on prices and policies, not on the generation mix, so D9 is computed before the year loop, backwards from the last year.

2. Method (with fixes)

H Demand. Four demand groups (transport, residential, industry, services), with electricity elasticities to income, usage, efficiency and autonomous efficiency (eps_Y, eps_U, eps_F, alpha). - Each group’s price is last year’s residential and industrial prices weighted 0.5/0.5 for transport, 1/0 for residential, 0/1 for industry and services. - Each group’s demand multiple combines autonomous efficiency, GDP growth (with the income factor), the price change and an optional shock: (1 / (1 + alpha + extra))^(1 + eps_U) × (1 + GDP growth)^(eps_Y × inc) × (price ratio)^(eps_U + eps_F + eps_U eps_F) × (1 + shock × switch). - Demand of each of the 15 subsectors = last year’s × its group’s multiple (services use the services multiple in every year). - Required generation (MWy) = demand × (1 + net exports + transmission + own use + statistical difference shares), converted from ktoe. In the base year it is actual generation. - Memo: demand by group, and electricity demand by subsector, sector and in total.

A0 Data of the scenario. - Capacity factor = actual (base-year output / installed capacity) when strictly between the CFOverride bounds of the scenario (wind and solar: CFOverrideIfBelowWndSol; others: CFOverrideIfBelowFosOth; upper CFOverrideIfAbove), else the studies value. Efficiency = actual (output / input) unless below MinimumThermalEfficiency (or without input), else the studies value. - WACC: Country* (country WACC + coal premium), Tech (country WACC + technology premium), else MAX(WACCUserGlobal (User), the income-group WACC (Income) or the studies WACC, MinimumWACC). Discounted lifetime = (1 − (1 + WACC)^−N) / (1 − (1 + WACC)^−1); renewables use the average of wind and solar. - New investment: the type’s NewInv setting; “If Present” = Yes when the type has more than 0.1% of base-year capacity; from the NewInv year (hydro from the base year when its generation share is above one half). - Scale-up rates: coal, gas, hydro MaxNonVREAsPCOfTotalGen; wind and solar the country default (CtryDefault), MaxWindSolarScaleup (UserDefined) or the category rate (Low, Medium, High, Vhigh); the others MaxNonNucOreBioAsPCOfTotalGen. Retirement rate 1/N, hydro 0 if HydroDoesntRetire = Yes. - Capex time factors of the decline scenario of RenewableCostDeclineRate (renewables; others medium); where the Power tab has no series for the option (Low and Vhigh for wind and solar, Vhigh for other renewables), the older series of the same option, else medium.

I Dispatch. - Fixed-output types (wind, solar, hydro, other renewables, oil, nuclear, biomass) generate capacity × capacity factor. - Coal and gas cover the rest. Each first gets the minimum it must supply given the other’s maximum (maximum = capacity × MaximumCoalCF for coal, × MaximumGasCF for gas; 0.9 by default); the remainder is shared by a logit EXP(-kDispatch × variable cost / lowest variable cost), capped at the maximum. - PPAs: a share p of coal and gas generation (PPAProportionCoalGas × phase-out factor) runs at its capacity factor, scaled to the need, whatever its cost: generation = logit dispatch × (1 − p) + effective capacity × (coal and gas need / coal and gas effective capacity) × p. The phase-out factor falls linearly to 0 over YearsToPhaseOutPPAs years after YearPPACoalGas when the switch of the scenario (PhaseOutCoalAndGasPPAsBaseline in scenario 1, PhaseOutCoalAndGasPPAsPolicy in the others) is Yes or Yes*; else 1. - Fuel use = generation / efficiency (ktoe); CO2 = fuel use × EF.

K Capacity and retirement. - Retirement = planned retirement (data in nameplate MW per year, if the type’s switch is Yes) or else capacity / lifetime; plus early retirement for coal; at most the capacity. Hydro does not retire. Effective retirement = retirement × capacity factor. - For Egypt the legacy file has no planned-retirement data, so every switch is No. - Early coal retirement: room = MAX(0, maximum wind and solar additions − last year’s increase in generation − last year’s non-coal effective retirements); early = MAX(0, MIN(0.8 × coal effective capacity, room) × (1 − coal share of a logit over coal variable cost and wind and solar investment costs)) / coal capacity factor × factor × (1 − PPA share). The factor is MaxCostBasedEarlyRetirement, and 0 in scenario 1 unless EarlyRetirementInBaseline = Yes (legacy F3993); plants under PPA do not retire early. - Capacity needed = MAX(required generation − (effective capacity − effective retirement), 0). Capacity bought in year t is available in t+1.

L Investment. - First round, permitted types only: logit share EXP(-2 × E1 / lowest permitted E1), limited by a scale-up limit (rate × total effective capacity). - Second round: the remaining deficit goes to types with space left, cheapest first. - Final deficit: with PwDeficitGas = Yes (Settings C45; default No, as legacy’s typed switch E4248) the deficit left after the second round goes to gas (legacy row 4252). - Nameplate investment = effective / capacity factor; USD = nameplate × capital cost of the year. - phi = nameplate / (capacity + nameplate − retirement), the new-vintage share used in the vintage averages B2 and B6.

D5, D6, D9, E1. Each sector has three policy rates ($/tCO2), the same for the fuel blocks and power: - carbon tax = carbon price × sector coverage × (1 − ETS coverage); - ETS cost = ETS tax-equivalent price × ETS coverage × (1 − OBR share); - shadow price = feebate (and regulation) rate × share impacting efficiency + ETS tax-equivalent price × ETS coverage × OBR share.

New policy cost per kWh (D5) = tCO2/kWh × (carbon tax × fuel coverage + ETS cost) + fuel price reform per kWh. The levelised fixed cost (C) includes the renewable subsidy per kWh of the scenario (chapter Power 3.3). The shadow price per kWh is the power shadow price × tCO2/kWh; the rebate returns it in proportion to base-year shares, per kWh generated. Total variable cost = cost before new policies + new policy cost + shadow price + rebate. D9 = discounted average of the total variable cost to 2041, the last year (as legacy). E1 = MAX(levelised fixed cost + D9, 0.01).

3. Validation (check_report_v0.6.4.md)

  1. Method = legacy. The Python reference, fed with the cached legacy inputs and the bugs switched on, reproduces 208 legacy rows of the baseline and the carbon-tax run.
  2. Workbook = Python. With all bugs fixed and the prototype inputs, the workbook matches the Python reference for every power row, in every scenario test.
  3. Properties: generation = required generation; the capacity balance holds; investment is at most the capacity needed (scale-up limits can leave part unplaced); the supply cost lag holds; a carbon price raises the VRE share and lowers power CO2.
  4. Planned retirement. With the switch on for gas and oil and test data, the workbook matches the Python reference, and retirement equals the data. 4b. Power features. One test scenario per feature (check section 5d): MaximumGasCF and long-term storage; the power inputs of the scenario (two scenarios with many inputs changed, including WACC options, capacity-factor and efficiency bounds, permits, scale-up categories, decline options, storage, logit constants, pass-through, spot prices); PPAs; renewable subsidies; the rebate; the electricity excise and the manual excise; the final deficit to gas with tight scale-up limits. Each matches the Python reference.
  5. Memo lines equal power demand by subsector and add up by sector and in total.
  6. Totals. Fuel use, revenue and CO2 totals with power equal the Python reference; with non-zero existing carbon pricing test data, the power variable cost, revenues by type and totals match too. Check rows confirm that the totals equal their sector, power and fuel parts.

Power generation costs and power prices: method

Equations: Equations.md; workbook formulas: Formulas.md.

Scope. The price side of the power sector: - what each generation type costs per kWh; - what the generation mix costs on average; - the end-user electricity prices for residential and industrial users; - the power-sector carbon cost and its revenue, by user group and by generation type, and the power part of the revenue totals.

It follows legacy CPAT section 4 (“Technoeconomic (‘engineer’) power model”, Mitigation rows 2954-3672 and 3867-3927) and documentation chapter 3, sections 3.4.1 and 3.4.2.5, keeping legacy’s sub-table letters (A Inputs, B Amortised costs, C Levelised costs, D Variable costs, E Total costs, F Memos, G End-user prices, J Storage, plus M Revenues by type). The quantities (demand H, dispatch I, capacity K, investment L) come from the engineer model (chapter Power): generation shares, investment shares and consumption. A policy scenario’s power prices therefore respond to its own generation mix, investment and demand.

Units: real US dollars of the results year (2026) per kWh, unless stated. Every ScenarioDefinition power setting (pass-through, minimum cost, storage, WACC, capacity factor and efficiency bounds, renewable subsidy, rebate, excise) is read in the scenario’s own ScenarioDefinition column (chapter Power, inputs by scenario). Generation types f: coal, natural gas, oil, nuclear, wind, solar, hydro, other renewables, biomass (legacy order).

1. Interactions

The price block is mostly a one-way chain. The table lists its links with the rest of the model and how legacy keeps them free of circular references; the prototype implements all of them.

# From To What flows How the loop is broken (legacy)
I1 Fuel prices (coal and gas for power, oil, biomass) D Variable costs Fuel price before new policies ($/GJ, $/bbl) One way
I2 Policies (carbon price, ETS power coverage, fuel price reform) D Carbon cost; G end-user prices; revenues Carbon cost per kWh by generation type One way
I3 Generation mix (dispatch I, investment L) E average generation cost; J storage (via the VRE share) Generation shares by type Dispatch uses current variable costs, which do not depend on the average cost; prices use last year’s average cost (I5)
I4 Investment (L) B weighted average capex and storage cost of vintages New investment as a share of remaining capacity Φ B uses last year’s Φ (exact match with legacy, see 3.2)
I5 E average generation cost G end-user prices Supply cost = average generation cost + transmission Deliberately lagged one year: the price in t uses the cost of t−1
I6 G end-user prices Electricity demand → generation → I3 Residential and industrial electricity prices Through I5’s lag; demand (H) uses last year’s prices, by demand group
I7 J storage (systems cost of renewables) B (vintage storage cost) and C (levelised, for investment) Marginal storage cost of wind, solar, other renewables Uses the current VRE share from dispatch
I8 C levelised costs, D9 levelised variable costs Investment logit; F7 switching carbon prices Levelised total investment cost by type Investment only: not in end-user prices
I9 G revenues and M revenues by type Revenue totals USD million One way

Two consequences shape the design: 1. End-user prices depend on generation costs only through the weighted average (E) and the transmission add-on. Variable, amortised and storage costs matter for prices only as far as they enter that average. 2. The renewables systems cost is an interaction inside the price block. The VRE share sets the marginal storage cost (J), which enters the cost of wind and solar in two ways: amortised, through vintages, in the cost of the existing stock (B), which goes into end-user prices; and levelised, in the cost of new plants (C), which goes into investment. So a scenario that adds renewables raises their storage cost per kWh, which feeds back into the average generation cost a year later.

3.2 B. Amortised (cost-recovery) fixed costs: the cost of the existing stock

  • Weighted average capex of the stock: last year’s average and last year’s new capex, weighted by last year’s Φ; in the base year, the base-year capex. Exact match with legacy.
  • Weighted average storage cost of vintages: the same, but new vintages take the current-year marginal storage cost. Exact match with legacy.
  • Amortised fixed cost (S1) = (average capex × (1/N + wacc/2) + dtc/N) / (cf × 8,760) + average storage cost + opf.

3.3 C. Levelised (forward-looking) fixed costs: new plants (investment)

Levelised fixed cost (S2) = (cax + dtc + tcx) / (cf × 8,760 × dlf) + marginal storage cost + opf + rns.

3.3b Renewable subsidies (A17, C5)

  • Subsidy per kWh rns(t) = −(base subsidy RenewableSubsidyBL + additional subsidy RenSubsidyAddUSDkwh × MAX(0, 1 − (t − start)/n) from the start year RenSubsidyAddStartYear, policy scenarios only), with n = RenSubsidyAddPhaseoutYrs. The inputs are labelled nominal and used as real, as legacy.
  • It applies to wind, solar, other renewables and biomass, and to hydro if RenewableSubsidyHydro = Yes.
  • It enters the levelised fixed cost (investment), not the cost of the existing stock or the end-user price; its revenue is rns × generation by type (negative), in the power subsidies and in row ren of the revenue by policy.

3.4 D. Current variable costs

  • Fuel price for power (A18; v0.6.2, legacy bug L126): average of the fuel price before new policies fpn over t-4 .. t from the base year (one year in 2022, five from 2026); legacy averages t-2 .. t+2, which reads later years. The spot price is a switch. Row fpn holds this year’s price, so the average reads one row and only the scenario’s own years.
  • Fuel cost per kWh = fuel price × 0.0036 / ν. Nuclear: 0.004 $/kWh. Renewables 0.
  • Change in existing carbon pricing against the price data (chapter Prices section 5b): tCO2/kWh × the change in the existing carbon tax and ETS price, weighted by their coverage of power and the fuel (0 for Egypt).
  • Variable cost before new policies = variable O&M + fuel cost + change in existing carbon pricing.
  • Carbon cost per kWh generated = the new policy wedge on the fuel in power ($/GJ) × 0.0036 / ν. The wedge sums the carbon tax (carbon price × EF × fuel coverage × power coverage × (1 − ETS coverage of power)), the ETS (tax-equivalent price × EF × ETS coverage of power × (1 − OBR share of power)) and the fuel price reform for coal and gas in power.
  • Total variable cost = cost before new policies + carbon cost + shadow price + rebate (feebates and the output-based part of the ETS). Dispatch uses it.

3.5 E. Total costs

  • Generation cost before carbon (cost recovery, legacy E3) = amortised fixed cost + variable cost before new policies.
  • Average generation cost = generation-share-weighted average of that cost.
  • Average carbon cost per kWh consumed = share-weighted carbon cost × generation / consumption. The ratio covers losses, own use and net exports, from the base-year balance.

Carbon is kept out of the average generation cost and added separately (legacy, documentation 3.4.1.7), so a rebate can be computed against it.

3.6 F. Memos

F7 switching carbon prices, F1 system cost and F4 carbon intensity are memos. Only F4 (tCO2 per kWh = EF × 0.0036/ν) is built, as a parameter.

3.7 J. Systems integration cost of renewables (storage)

  • VRE share v = share of wind + solar + other renewables (legacy row 3851).
  • Short-term storage (batteries): marginal hours = ast × 18 × v, with ast = 1 the share allocated to VRE and 18 = d(9 v²)/dv. Cost per hour per kWh = (battery capex + interface capex / 2 hours kWh/kW) / (dlf of renewables × 8,760) + battery opex / 8,760.
  • Long-term storage (electrolysers): marginal units = LongTermStorageFor100pcVRE × 2 × max(0, v − x) / (1 − x)², with x = StartingPointWhenLTStorageIsNeeded (0.75). Cost per unit = (electrolyser capex + LT storage capex per kWh × StorageHoursForLTStorage) / (dlf of renewables × 8,760) + fixed opex / 8,760 + variable opex.
  • Marginal storage cost of wind, solar and other renewables = hours × cost per hour + units × cost per unit; 0 for other types.

Legacy has a 33% “allocation of LT storage to VRE” parameter and applies it twice (rows 3911 and 3912, a bug). The prototype applies it once.

3.8 G. End-user prices (residential and industrial)

  • Supply cost (I5, lagged) = last year’s average generation cost + transmission (0.040 $/kWh residential, 0.015 industrial). In the base year, the base-year average cost.
  • Price before new policies (S4): up to the last historical year L, the historical retail price including VAT from the price dataset (nominal → real with the CPI index); after L, last year’s value + pass-through × change in supply cost × (1 + VAT of L), with pass-through 1 (legacy hardcode).
  • End-user price = price before new policies + (average carbon cost × pass-through × (1 − rebate switch) + excise) × (1 + VAT). As in legacy (rows 3614-3617), VAT is charged on the new policy terms.
  • Rebate switch (legacy rows 261, 2188, J3595): 1 in policy scenarios with PowerRebates = Yes (or Yes*) or a feebate scenario of the legacy scenario table (Feebates, Power Supply Feebates, Power feebate); the carbon cost is then not passed to electricity users.
  • Excise per kWh before VAT = electricity excise + pass-through × (manual excise − its value in the last historical year) after that year. The electricity excise (scenario Electricity excise, policy scenarios; legacy row 2253) = grid CO2 intensity of the base year (CO2 of the fuel input to power / power output, Balances) × carbon price. The manual excise (policy scenarios with AdditionalExcise = Yes; legacy rows 3602, 3631) is the new electricity excise of ‘Manual inputs’ rows 161-162 (real, or nominal × the deflator index if NomorReal is not Real); like legacy it enters the price before policy, so up to the last historical year it is absorbed by the price data and later its change is passed through.
  • Subsidy gap (S7) = price before new policies / (1 + VAT) − supply cost. Negative = price below the modelled supply cost (subsidies are negative).

3.9 G4 and M. Revenues (USD million real) and the revenue totals

By user group (G), each × consumption of the group: - VAT = price before new policies × VAT / (1 + VAT); - VAT on the new-policy cost passed through = (average carbon cost × pass-through × (1 − rebate switch) + excise) × VAT; - rebate (negative) = − rebate switch × average carbon cost × pass-through: the carbon cost returned to users (the VAT on it is not collected); - excise = excise per kWh × consumption, of which the electricity excise of the scenario (carbon-based) and the manual excise; - subsidy gap (signed: negative = fiscal cost, positive when the price is above the modelled supply cost); - new-policy cost (memo; it values the ETS at the tax-equivalent price) = average carbon cost.

By generation type (M), with a fuel flag of 1 for coal, gas, oil and biomass: - existing taxes on generation fuels = flag × fuel use × (excise + VAT) of the price fuel; - existing subsidies on generation fuels = flag × fuel use × subsidy (negative); - change in existing carbon pricing × generation; - new policies = generation × carbon cost − CO2 × the power ETS cost rate × (1 − revenue factor): the ETS part at the permit price, as in the subsectors; - existing carbon pricing level (memo) = CO2 × the covered existing carbon tax and ETS prices.

The revenue totals add a power row: existing taxes = fuel taxes + change in existing carbon pricing + electricity VAT; subsidies = fuel subsidies + subsidy gap + renewable subsidies; new policies = new-policy revenue by type + VAT on new policies + rebate + excise; existing carbon pricing level = the memo by type. In the revenue by policy (12e), the rebate and the carbon-based excise count with the carbon tax, the manual excise with the excise, and the renewable subsidies in their own row (as legacy rows 7749, 7762, 7903, the base subsidy once). The fuel subsidy and the subsidy gap do not overlap: the supply cost already uses the subsidised fuel price, so the gap is what remains between that cost and the tariff.

8. Legacy bugs and oddities in these chains

  1. A2 rows 2981–2985 (wnd, sol, hyd, ore, bio), columns P..T: the IF(ISERROR(INDEX(K)),INDEX(K),IF(ISERROR(INDEX(L)),INDEX(L),INDEX(M))) logic is inverted, so the result is always the world value.   3. Hydro investment start (H4067): IF($L$3827>50%, …) tests a year header (2022 > 0.5), so hydro always starts in the first year (2022 instead of NewInvHydYear = 2030).
  4. Regional/country code shadowing: MATCH over Power!A25:A356 finds the regional rows (42–109) first for chn.mpp.*, jpn.mpp.* and kor.mpp.*. For China, Japan and Korea, region data replace country data (18 codes).
  5. A7 country ctf code iso.med.ctf.t uses a different segment order from the world code wld.ctf.med.t, and there are no country/regional ctf rows, so the country branch is dead.
  6. D2 nuclear fuel: L3353 = K2727 (shifted one column: 2022 takes the 2021 value). The value is the typed constant Mitigation!K710:AE710 = 0.004, not Power!G22. No numeric effect.
