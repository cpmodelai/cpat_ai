8  Distribution

The distribution module shows who bears the cost of a scenario. It takes the price changes and the revenue that mitigation computes for one year, the analysis year, and turns them into a change in the consumption of households, decile by decile. It then gives the revenue back to households through the recycling options chosen in the scenario and reports what is left: the net effect, the inequality it implies and the sectors whose prices rise most.

The methodology describes the module in methodology, chapter 5; its overview of functions is in section 5.1.1.2. This chapter is the model as Excel-AI and CPAT Python compute it, with the places where it simplifies or departs from the methodology.

8.1 The module at a glance

The module works on one scenario at a time and always against scenario 1, the baseline. Every result has eleven values: the basket (the whole survey sample) and the ten consumption deciles, from the poorest (decile 1) to the wealthiest (decile 10). Most results are given for three samples: overall, urban and rural.

The calculation runs in eleven steps, the sections of the Distribution sheet.

Section What it computes Equations
1 Settings The scenario’s distribution options and the programmes they select
2 Inputs from mitigation Price changes by fuel, base-year spending, GDP, population, new revenue D1.3
3 Indirect prices Price change of 14 consumption categories through the GTAP input-output table D1.2, B1.1
4 Price factors Price change with the elasticity adjustment and the cooking-fuel exemption D1.4, B1.4
5 Budget shares Survey budget shares by sample, statistic and decile D1.1
6 Effects Direct, indirect and total effect in % of consumption D1.4, B1.3
7 Rebasing Survey population and consumption scaled to national totals B1.2
8 Recycling Income-tax cut, public investment, current spending, targeted transfer D1.5, B1.5, B1.6
9 Net effect Net effect, compensation shares, Gini before and after recycling D1.6, B1.7
10 Chart outputs Labels, amounts per person, Lorenz curves, percentile households
11 Production sectors Input-cost and output price change of each GTAP sector, top 20, 14 CPAT sectors B1.8

Budget shares (section 5) come before the price factors (section 4) in the code, because the weighted summary of the price factors uses them; the rebasing (section 7) comes before the effects (section 6), because the mitigation adjustment of section 6 needs national consumption. The order of the sections in the sheet is kept for reading.

8.1.1 Notation

The module uses the indices of methodology chapter 5. Every equation holds for one country, one scenario and the analysis year \(y\); these indices are omitted.

Symbol Meaning
\(d\) Decile, 1 (poorest) to 10; the basket is written \(d = 0\)
\(s\) Sample: overall, urban, rural (in Section 8.12: CPAT sector)
\(f\) Household fuel: coal, electricity, natural gas, other oil products, gasoline, diesel, kerosene, LPG
\(g\) Consumption category (14, Table 8.2)
\(i\) Item: a fuel or a category; in Section 8.5 and Section 8.12, a GTAP sector
\(k\) GTAP fuel group: coal, electricity, gas, crude oil, petroleum and coke products
\(y\) Analysis year, input YearDistn
\(B\), \(P\) Scenario 1 (baseline), the scenario under study

Price changes are fractions (0.18 is 18%). Budget shares, effects and recycling gains are in percent of consumption. Amounts are in local currency.

8.2 Settings

Each scenario carries its own distribution settings. They choose the analysis year, the survey statistic, the optional adjustments of the price effect and how the revenue is recycled.

The percentages are entered in percent (30 means 30%) and divided by 100 where they are used.

Four values are derived from the settings:

  • Infrastructure-access variable, from PublicInfrastructureInvType: All Infr. (all_acs_share), ICT (ICT_acs_share), Electricity (ely_acs_share), Water (wtr_acs_share), Sanitation (sani_acs_share), Public Transport (transp_pub_acs_share).
  • ASPIRE programme code, from CurrentSpendingType and from TransferType: Active Labor Market, All Labor Market, All Social Assistance, All Social Insurance, All Social Protection and Labor, Cash Transfers, Conditional Cash Transfers, Contributory Pensions, Fee waivers and targeted subsidies, In-Kind, Other Social Assistance, Other Social Insurance, Passive Labor Market, Public Works, School Feeding, Social Pensions.
  • Most-used cooking fuel of the country, from the WHO Household Energy Database (the fossil fuel with the largest share of the population cooking with it).
  • Targeting mode of the transfer: ASPIRE when TransferType names a programme with data for the country; Cash when it is Cash (or a programme without data) and TargetBelow is No; PL (poverty line) when it is Cash and TargetBelow names a line.

A support check reports OK, or names the option that falls back: “TransferType: no ASPIRE data, cash used”.

8.3 Inputs from mitigation

The module needs five things from mitigation: the price change of each household fuel, the economy-wide price change of each fuel, what the economy spent on each fuel, GDP and population, and the revenue to recycle. All are read for the analysis year, in the scenario and in scenario 1. The methodology describes the link in section 5.3.1.

8.3.1 Household price change by fuel

The direct effect uses the residential price, the price households pay (M2.5, Chapter 2):

\[ t_f = \frac{p^{P}_{f,\text{res},y}}{p^{B}_{f,\text{res},y}} - 1 \tag{8.1}\]

where

  • \(t_f\) is the household price change of fuel \(f\) (fraction; code dpr.<f>.res);
  • \(p_{f,\text{res},y}\) is the residential after-tax price atp of fuel \(f\) in the analysis year (local unit); for electricity, the residential retail price rp.res.ecy;
  • \(P\) and \(B\) mark the scenario and scenario 1.

This is D1.3. Oil (oil in the survey) takes the price of other oil products (oop).

8.3.2 Economy-wide price change and spending

The indirect effect uses the price that producers pay. It is the change in the average price over sectors (rpn, Chapter 2):

\[ t^{a}_f = \frac{\bar{p}^{P}_{f,y}}{\bar{p}^{B}_{f,y}} - 1 \tag{8.2}\]

where

  • \(t^{a}_f\) is the economy-wide price change of fuel \(f\) (code dpa.<f>);
  • \(\bar{p}_{f,y}\) is the average price rpn of the industry sector for coal and natural gas, of all sectors for other oil products, gasoline, diesel, LPG and kerosene, and the industrial retail price rp.ind.ecy for electricity.

Each fuel is weighted by what the economy spent on it, in scenario 1 and in the base year:

\[ S_f = \text{pav.sp}^{B}_{f,t_0} \times \begin{cases} 0.041868 & f \neq \text{electricity} \\ 1 & f = \text{electricity} \end{cases} \tag{8.3}\]

where

  • \(S_f\) is the base-year spending on fuel \(f\) (USD million; code spend.<f>);
  • \(\text{pav.sp}\) is the economy-wide spending on the fuel, all sectors, from the price block (Chapter 2);
  • \(0.041868\) is PJ per ktoe, which turns the fuels’ spending into USD million (electricity’s already is);
  • \(t_0\) is the base year. The spending of the base year is paired with the price changes of the analysis year (issue I76, below).

8.3.3 Macro inputs

Input Formula Code
GDP \(GDP_y\) = scenario gdp.lcu × \(10^9\) (local currency) gdp
Population \(POP_y\) = scenario pop × 1000 pop
Revenue to recycle \(R\) = scenario rnew × \(10^6\) × xr (local currency) rev

The revenue is the revenue from new policies (M6.1, Chapter 7): carbon tax, the VAT on it and auctioned ETS permits. It stands for legacy’s carbon-pricing revenue (issue I80).

8.4 Budget shares

A household loses more from a price rise the more of its budget goes to the good. The budget shares come from household budget surveys, prepared as the methodology describes in section 5.2.1 (eq-5_1):

\[ \pi^{g}_{j} = \frac{C^{g}_{j}}{C_j}, \qquad \pi^{dg} = \underset{j \in d}{\text{mean, median, p25 or p75}}\ \pi^{g}_{j} \tag{8.4}\]

where

  • \(\pi^{g}_{j}\) is household \(j\)’s share of its consumption \(C_j\) spent on item \(g\);
  • \(\pi^{dg}\) is the population-weighted statistic over the households of decile \(d\) (percent; code bsh.<g>.dec.<s>).

This is D1.1. The survey tables (HHSurvey) already hold the decile statistics; the model reads them by sample (overall, urban, rural), by the statistic QuantilesStatistic and by decile. The basket reads the whole-sample row (quantile code 9999).

The items are the eight household fuels, the 14 consumption categories and, in the overall sample, three biomass fuels (charcoal, ethanol, firewood). Biomass has a budget share but no price change. The energy budget share, fuels plus biomass, is the summary row of the section.

Not every survey row follows the chosen statistic. Population weights, consumption per person, infrastructure access and the poverty-line shares are always read from the mean rows; income-tax shares and the PIT-to-GDP ratio follow the statistic.

8.5 Indirect price changes through the input-output table

A carbon price also raises the price of food, transport and everything else made with energy. The module finds these indirect price changes with the GTAP input-output table of the country: how much of each fuel group is embedded, directly and upstream, in one unit of each sector’s output. The methodology describes the approach in section 5.3.1 (eq-5_3) and the scaling in section 5.3.2.4.

The table has 64 GTAP sectors: five fuel groups and 59 non-fuel sectors, which map to the 14 categories. CPAT’s eight fuels map to the five GTAP fuel groups:

8.5.1 Step 1: the price factor of each fuel group

First, the spending-weighted price change of each group, and the group’s total use:

\[ R_k = \sum_{f \in k} S_f\, t^{a}_f \tag{8.5}\]

\[ U_k = \frac{D_{t_R}}{D_{GTAP}} \Big( \sum_{i \notin \text{fuels}} (TD_i + X_i)\, L_{i,k} + TD_k \Big) \tag{8.6}\]

where

  • \(R_k\) is the extra cost of group \(k\) at base-year spending (USD million; code grev.<k>);
  • \(U_k\) is the total use of group \(k\): embedded in the total demand \(TD_i\) and exports \(X_i\) of the non-fuel sectors, plus the group’s own demand \(TD_k\) (USD million; code gtot.<k>, DIOTot on DistMapping);
  • \(L_{i,k}\) is GTAP’s total-requirement (Leontief) coefficient of group \(k\) in sector \(i\);
  • \(D_{t_R} / D_{GTAP}\) is the WEO GDP deflator of the results year over that of the GTAP year (issue I77).

Equation 8.6 is B1.1. The price factor of the group is

\[ \phi_k = \begin{cases} R_k / U_k & \texttt{ScaleGTAP} = \text{Yes} \\[2pt] R_k / \sum_{f \in k} S_f & \texttt{ScaleGTAP} = \text{No}\ (0 \text{ without spending}) \end{cases} \tag{8.7}\]

where \(\phi_k\) is the factor applied to the Leontief coefficients of group \(k\) (code gfac.<k>).

With ScaleGTAP = Yes the extra cost CPAT computes for the fuels is spread over everything the group is used for. With ScaleGTAP = No, \(\phi_k\) is simply the spending-weighted economy-wide price change of the group’s fuels.

8.5.2 Step 2: the price change of each category

\[ t_g = \sum_k \bar{L}_{g,k}\, \phi_k, \qquad \bar{L}_{g,k} = \frac{\sum_{i \in g} H_i\, L_{i,k}}{\sum_{i \in g} H_i} \tag{8.8}\]

where

  • \(t_g\) is the price change of category \(g\) (fraction; code dpr.<g>);
  • \(\bar{L}_{g,k}\) is the household-demand weighted Leontief coefficient of the category (DLcat on DistMapping);
  • \(H_i\) is GTAP household demand for the output of sector \(i\).

This is D1.2. The summary row of the section is the budget-share weighted average of \(t_g\) over the categories, overall sample.

TipPseudocode: indirect price changes 
# data step (once per country)
ratio = weo_deflator[results_year] / weo_deflator[gtap_year]
for k in [coa, ely, nga, oil, p_c]:
    gtot[k] = ratio * (sum((TD[i] + X[i]) * L[i, k] for i in nonfuel_sectors) + TD[k])
    for g in categories:
        DLcat[g, k] = sum(H[i] * L[i, k] for i in g) / sum(H[i] for i in g)

# each scenario
for k in groups:
    grev[k]   = sum(spend[f] * dpa[f] for f in fuels_of[k])
    gspend[k] = sum(spend[f] for f in fuels_of[k])
    if ScaleGTAP == 'Yes':
        gfac[k] = grev[k] / gtot[k]
    else:
        gfac[k] = grev[k] / gspend[k] if gspend[k] > 0 else 0
for g in categories:
    dpr[g] = sum(DLcat[g, k] * gfac[k] for k in groups)
NoteWhy one factor per fuel group is legacy’s rescaling

Legacy CPAT (with ScaleGTAP = Yes, its default) rescales the raw price change of each GTAP sector \(s\) by

\[ \kappa_s = \frac{\sum_k \sigma_{k,s}\, R_k}{\sum_k Emb_{k,s}\, t_{k,s}}, \]

where \(Emb_{k,s}\) is the use of group \(k\) embedded in sector \(s\), \(\sigma_{k,s}\) its share in the group’s total use and \(t_{k,s}\) the price change of the fuel for that sector. With \(Emb_{k,s} = Y_s L_{s,k}\) and \(\sigma_{k,s} = Emb_{k,s} / U_k\), the sector’s adjusted price change becomes

\[ \kappa_s \sum_k L_{s,k}\, t_{k,s} = \sum_k L_{s,k}\, \frac{R_k}{U_k} = \sum_k L_{s,k}\, \phi_k . \]

The sector-specific fuel prices cancel. The model therefore keeps one factor per group. Checked on legacy’s cache: all 59 sectors agree to 4e-16, and the 14 categories to 3e-6 (legacy prints six digits).

8.6 Price factors

A price change costs a household its budget share times the change, if it buys the same quantities. When it buys less, the cost is smaller. The price factor carries this behavioural response; it is the methodology’s decile-specific price elasticity of demand (section 5.3.2.2, eq-5_7):

\[ \psi_{d,i} = \begin{cases} t_i\, (1 + t_i)^{\epsilon_{d,i}} & \texttt{PEDs} = \text{Yes} \\ t_i & \texttt{PEDs} = \text{No} \end{cases} \tag{8.9}\]

where

  • \(\psi_{d,i}\) is the price factor of item \(i\) in decile \(d\) (fraction; code fac.<i>);
  • \(t_i\) is the household price change of a fuel (Equation 8.1) or of a category (Equation 8.8);
  • \(\epsilon_{d,i}\) is the decile’s own-price elasticity of item \(i\) (HH_Elast; code eps.<i>). The basket uses the basket elasticity.

\(t_i (1 + t_i)^{\epsilon}\) is the price change times the quantity the household still buys: with a negative elasticity the factor is below \(t_i\).

8.6.1 Cooking-fuel exemption

To keep the poorest households from switching back to firewood or charcoal, the scenario can exempt their main cooking fuel (section 5.3.2.5). In policy terms it is a rebate: the household pays the tax and gets it back.

\[ \psi_{d,f^c} = 0 \qquad \text{for } 1 \le d \le \bar{d}, \text{ when } \texttt{ExemptMostCook} = \text{Yes} \tag{8.10}\]

where

  • \(f^c\) is the country’s most-used fossil cooking fuel (WHO Household Energy Database; LPG for Egypt);
  • \(\bar{d}\) is ExemptCookDeciles (default 4).

This is B1.4. The basket is never exempt. The summary row of the section is the budget-share weighted average of the fuel price factors, overall sample.

TipPseudocode: price factors 
for i in fuels + categories:
    dp = dpr[i + '.res'] if i in fuels else dpr[i]       # oil reads dpr['oop.res']
    for d in [basket, 1, ..., 10]:
        fac[i][d] = dp * (1 + dp) ** eps[i][d] if PEDs == 'Yes' else dp
        if i == cooking_fuel and ExemptMostCook == 'Yes' and 1 <= d <= ExemptCookDeciles:
            fac[i][d] = 0
facw = sum(fac[f] * bsh[f, 'ove'] for f in fuels) / sum(bsh[f, 'ove'] for f in fuels)   # summary

8.7 Rebasing the survey to national accounts

Survey totals rarely match national accounts, and the survey year is not the analysis year. The module scales the survey population to the projected population and survey consumption to private consumption in national accounts (section 5.3.2.1, eq-5_5, eq-5_11).

\[ C^{NA}_y = GDP_y \times \frac{\theta_c}{100} \tag{8.11}\]

\[ N_{d,s} = n_{d,s}\, \frac{POP_y}{n_0}, \qquad c_{d,s} = c^{svy}_{d,s}\, \frac{C^{NA}_y}{c^{svy}_0\, POP_y}, \qquad X_{d,s} = c_{d,s}\, N_{d,s} \tag{8.12}\]

where

  • \(C^{NA}_y\) is national private consumption in the analysis year (local currency; code nacons);
  • \(\theta_c\) is households’ final consumption in % of GDP, WDI NE.CON.PRVT.ZS, in its latest year (code consgdp; Egypt: 82.6% in 2023);
  • \(n_{d,s}\) is the survey population weight of decile \(d\) in sample \(s\) (popw), \(n_0\) that of the whole overall sample;
  • \(c^{svy}_{d,s}\) is survey consumption per person including actual rent (cons_pc_acrent), \(c^{svy}_0\) that of the overall basket;
  • \(N_{d,s}\), \(c_{d,s}\) and \(X_{d,s}\) are the decile’s population, consumption per person and total consumption, scaled (codes pop_tot_adj, cons_pc_na, tot_cons_na).

This is B1.2. The two scaling factors (codes popadj and consadj) come from the overall sample and apply to urban and rural alike. The summary row of the section is total consumption.

TipPseudocode: survey to national accounts 
nacons  = gdp * consgdp / 100
popadj  = pop / popw['ove'][basket]
consadj = nacons / cons_pc_svy['ove'][basket] / pop
for s in [ove, urb, rur]:
    for d in [basket, 1, ..., 10]:
        pop_tot_adj[s][d] = popw[s][d] * popadj
        cons_pc_na[s][d]  = cons_pc_svy[s][d] * consadj
        tot_cons_na[s][d] = cons_pc_na[s][d] * pop_tot_adj[s][d]

8.8 Direct, indirect and total effect

The effect of the price changes on a decile is the budget-share weighted sum of its price factors, as a loss (negative) in percent of consumption. Fuels give the direct effect, categories the indirect effect (eq-5_2). The methodology’s corrections of 2026-10-31 (DOC16) make the direct effect refer to eq-5_2, with \(f \in g\).

\[ e^{dir}_{d,s} = -\lambda \sum_{f} \psi_{d,f}\, \pi^{df}_s, \qquad e^{ind}_{d,s} = -\lambda \sum_{g} \psi_{d,g}\, \pi^{dg}_s, \qquad e_{d,s} = e^{dir}_{d,s} + e^{ind}_{d,s} \tag{8.13}\]

where

  • \(e^{dir}\), \(e^{ind}\), \(e\) are the direct, indirect and total effect (percent of consumption, a loss is negative; codes def.tot, ief.tot, tef.tot; by item def.<f>, ief.<g>);
  • \(\pi^{di}_s\) is the budget share of item \(i\) in decile \(d\) of sample \(s\) (Equation 8.4);
  • \(\lambda\) is the loss scale (Equation 8.15), 1 unless an adjustment is chosen.

This is D1.4 (there written as the burden \(p_d = -e_d\)). The urban and rural samples use their own budget shares with the overall price factors. Without PEDs these are Laspeyres (upper-bound) estimates: households keep their quantities.

8.8.1 Mitigation adjustment and deadweight loss

The input-output effect assumes that households and firms keep buying the same quantities. Mitigation, which lets them adjust, raises less revenue. With InferredDist the effect is scaled down to the revenue mitigation actually raises (section 5.3.2.1, eq-5_4, eq-5_6). First, the revenue the household effect implies:

\[ P_D = \sum_{d=1}^{10} \Big( \sum_i \psi_{d,i}\, \pi^{di} \Big) \frac{X_d}{100} \tag{8.14}\]

Then the share mitigation raises, and the loss scale:

\[ \beta = \begin{cases} \min\!\big(1, \max(0, R / P_D)\big) & \texttt{InferredDist} = \text{Yes} \\ 1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \qquad \lambda = \beta + \begin{cases} \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - \beta) & \texttt{DWLs} = \text{Yes} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \tag{8.15}\]

where

  • \(P_D\) is the implied revenue, overall sample (local currency; code revimpl);
  • \(R\) is the new revenue of mitigation (Section 8.3.3);
  • \(\beta\) is the behavioural factor (code badj), \(1 - p_{Adj}\) in the methodology’s terms;
  • \(\tfrac{1}{2}\) is the Harberger triangle (constant DWLTri): the deadweight loss is half the lost demand times the price change, added back to the households’ loss;
  • \(\lambda\) is the loss scale (code lscale).

This is B1.3. Since the deadweight loss is a share of the gap \(1 - \beta\), DWLs has no effect unless InferredDist is Yes.

TipPseudocode: effects 
for s in [ove, urb, rur]:
    uloss[s] = sum(fac[i] * bsh[i, s] for i in fuels + categories)
revimpl = sum(uloss['ove'][d] * tot_cons_na['ove'][d] for d in 1..10) / 100
badj    = min(1, max(0, rev / revimpl)) if InferredDist == 'Yes' else 1
lscale  = badj + (DWLTri * (1 - badj) if DWLs == 'Yes' else 0)
for s in [ove, urb, rur]:
    for f in fuels:      def[f, s] = -fac[f] * bsh[f, s] * lscale
    for g in categories: ief[g, s] = -fac[g] * bsh[g, s] * lscale
    def['tot', s] = sum(def[f, s] for f in fuels)
    ief['tot', s] = sum(ief[g, s] for g in categories)
    tef['tot', s] = def['tot', s] + ief['tot', s]                     # = -uloss[s] * lscale

8.9 Revenue recycling

The revenue \(R\) goes back to households in four ways, each with its share of the revenue. The methodology describes them in section 5.4. All four are computed as an amount per person by decile, in local currency, then turned into percent of each sample’s consumption.

\[ R_{PIT} = R\,\frac{\sigma_{PIT}}{100}, \quad R_{PUI} = R\,\frac{\sigma_{PUI}}{100}, \quad R_{CTS} = R\,\frac{\sigma_{CTS}}{100}, \quad R_{T} = R\,\frac{\sigma_{T}}{100} \tag{8.16}\]

where \(\sigma_{PIT}\), \(\sigma_{PUI}\), \(\sigma_{CTS}\), \(\sigma_T\) are EXPLabortax, EXPCapex, EXPGoodsandserv and EXPTransfers (percent; defaults 30, 50, 0, 20) and \(R_{\cdot}\) the amounts (codes rpit, rpui, rcts, rtgt). Let \(N = \sum_{d=1}^{10} N_d\) be the overall population (code poptot).

The amounts per person come from the overall sample; urban and rural deciles receive the same amount per person, except for public investment, which follows each sample’s access (issue I78).

8.9.1 Personal income-tax cut

Part of the revenue can cut the personal income tax (PIT) households pay (section 5.4.3). First, the PIT each decile pays per person:

\[ B = GDP_y\, \frac{\tau_{PIT}}{100}, \qquad \ell_d = \frac{B\, s_d}{\sum_{d'} s_{d'}\; N_d} \tag{8.17}\]

where

  • \(B\) is economy-wide PIT (local currency; code pitbase);
  • \(\tau_{PIT}\) is the PIT-to-GDP ratio of the country (percent; survey variable indv, code pitind);
  • \(s_d\) is the decile’s share of PIT paid (survey variable pit_share_income or pit_share_region by MissingDataReplacement; code pitsh);
  • \(\ell_d\) is PIT paid per person in decile \(d\) (code pitpc).

Then one of three cuts, chosen by PITReductionType:

\[ PIT_d = \begin{cases} \min(\bar{r}, \ell_d) + \dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{d'} (\bar{r} - \ell_{d'})^{+} N_{d'} & \text{PA} \\[8pt] \ell_d\, \dfrac{R_{PIT}}{B} & \text{PC} \\[8pt] \min\!\Big(\ell_d, \dfrac{(R_{PIT} - \Lambda_{d-1})^{+}}{N_d}\Big)\ (d \le \bar{d}_{PIT}),\ 0 \text{ above} & \text{TE} \end{cases} \tag{8.18}\]

where

  • \(PIT_d\) is the PIT cut per person in decile \(d\) (local currency; code pit); PA is Personal Allowance (code pitpa), PC Proportional Compensation (pitprop), TE Targeted Exemption (pitte);
  • \(\bar{r} = R_{PIT} / N\) is the most each person can get under the allowance (code pitmax); the second term shares out equally what the deciles that pay less than \(\bar{r}\) leave over (code pitextra);
  • \(\Lambda_{d-1} = \sum_{d' < d} \ell_{d'} N_{d'}\) is the PIT paid by the deciles below \(d\) (code pitcum);
  • \(\bar{d}_{PIT}\) is DistExcCfDec (default 4): the exemption works from the bottom up while revenue lasts.

This is B1.5. The personal allowance is like a lump sum to taxpayers and is progressive; the proportional cut follows the PIT schedule and is relatively regressive.

TipPseudocode: income-tax cut 
rpit    = rev * EXPLabortax / 100
poptot  = sum(N[d] for d in 1..10)
pitbase = gdp * pitind / 100
for d in 1..10:
    pitpc[d] = pitbase * pitsh[d] / sum(pitsh) / N[d]
pitmax   = rpit / poptot
pitextra = sum(max(pitmax - pitpc[d], 0) * N[d] for d in 1..10) / poptot
cum = 0
for d in 1..10:
    pitpa[d]   = min(pitmax, pitpc[d]) + pitextra
    pitprop[d] = pitpc[d] * rpit / pitbase
    pitte[d]   = min(pitpc[d], max(rpit - cum, 0) / N[d]) if d <= DistExcCfDec else 0
    cum += pitpc[d] * N[d]
    pit[d] = {'Personal Allowance': pitpa, 'Proportional Compensation': pitprop}.get(
                 PITReductionType, pitte)[d]

8.9.2 Targeted transfers

A targeted transfer goes to the bottom of the distribution. The scenario sets the targeted percentile, how many of the targeted receive (coverage) and how many of the others receive by mistake (leakage) (section 5.4.1, eq-5_9 to eq-5_12). The model builds it in three steps.

The targeted share of each decile. Deciles are not exactly 10% of the population once weighted, so the share of each decile inside the bottom percentile follows the cumulative population:

\[ n^{sh}_d = \frac{N_d}{N}, \qquad \tau_d = \max\!\Big(0,\ \min\big(n^{sh}_d,\ \tfrac{p_T}{100} - \textstyle\sum_{d' \le d} n^{sh}_{d'} + n^{sh}_d\big)\Big) \tag{8.19}\]

where \(n^{sh}_d\) is the decile’s population share (code popsh), \(p_T\) the targeted percentile TargettedPercentile (percent; default 40) and \(\tau_d\) the targeted share of the population, in the decile (code tgtsh).

The share of the decile that receives.

\[ r_d = \frac{\tau_d\, cov + (n^{sh}_d - \tau_d)\, leak}{100\; n^{sh}_d} \tag{8.20}\]

where \(cov\) and \(leak\) are CoverageRate and LeakageRate (percent; defaults 100 and 0) and \(r_d\) the share of the decile that receives (code recv).

The transfer. One formula serves the three targeting modes, through a weight \(w_d\):

\[ T_d = T\, r_d\, w_d\, \mu_d, \qquad T = \frac{R_T}{\sum_{d=1}^{10} r_d\, w_d\, N_d} \tag{8.21}\]

\[ w_d = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{Cash} \\ \min(10\, b^{PL}_d,\ 100) / 100 & \text{PL (poverty line)} \\ A_{\lceil d/2 \rceil} & \text{ASPIRE} \end{cases} \tag{8.22}\]

\[ \mu_d = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{median statistic and } r_d\, w^{PL}_d \le \tfrac{1}{2} \\ 1 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \tag{8.23}\]

where

  • \(T_d\) is the transfer per person in decile \(d\) (local currency; code tgt) and \(T\) the amount per weighted recipient (code tgtamt);
  • \(b^{PL}_d\) is the percentage of decile \(d\) below the poverty line TargetBelow (survey pct_belowpl_215, pct_belowpl_365 or pct_belowpl_685: lines of 2.15, 3.65 and 6.85 USD a day); times 10 it is the share of the decile below the line if deciles hold 10% of the sample (constant PlDecScale), capped at 100% (code plsh);
  • \(A_q\) is the ASPIRE benefit per person of the TransferType programme in quintile \(q = \lceil d/2 \rceil\) (constant DecPerQuint = 2; PPP USD a day, as in the data; 0 when absent; code aspt);
  • \(w^{PL}_d\) is the poverty-line share (1 outside PL mode) and \(r_d w^{PL}_d\) the share of the decile that receives (code rshare);
  • \(\mu_d\) is the median flag (code medflag): with the median statistic the representative household of a decile receives the transfer only if more than half the decile receives.

This is D1.5. The denominator makes the payout equal the revenue share exactly, in every mode, except that money the median flag withholds is not redistributed (as legacy). Poverty-line targeting works only with TransferType = Cash. A programme without ASPIRE data for the country falls back to cash, and the support check says so.

TipPseudocode: targeted transfer 
rtgt = rev * EXPTransfers / 100
popcum = 0
for d in 1..10:
    popsh[d] = N[d] / poptot
    popcum  += popsh[d]
    tgtsh[d] = max(0, min(popsh[d], TargettedPercentile / 100 - popcum + popsh[d]))
    recv[d]  = (tgtsh[d] * CoverageRate + (popsh[d] - tgtsh[d]) * LeakageRate) / 100 / popsh[d]
    q        = ceil(d / DecPerQuint)
    aspt[d]  = aspire.get(f'{iso}.{tgtcode}{q}_preT_tot', 0)
    plsh[d]  = min(PlDecScale * pct_belowpl[d], 100) / 100 if tgtmode == 'PL' else 1
    tgtw[d]  = aspt[d] if tgtmode == 'ASPIRE' else plsh[d]
    rshare   = recv[d] * plsh[d]
    medflag[d] = 0 if QuantilesStatistic == 'median' and rshare <= 1 - rshare else 1
den    = sum(recv[d] * tgtw[d] * N[d] for d in 1..10)
tgtamt = rtgt / den if den else 0
for d in 1..10:
    tgt[d] = tgtamt * recv[d] * tgtw[d] * medflag[d]

8.9.3 Public investment and current spending

Public investment reaches the people who lack the chosen infrastructure: water, sanitation, electricity, ICT, public transport, or all of them (section 5.2.2, eq-5_13). Its value is counted as cash. Current spending scales up an existing social-protection programme in proportion to what each quintile receives from it today (section 5.4.2, eq-5_14).

\[ PUI_{d,s} = \frac{R_{PUI}}{\sum_{d'} (1 - a_{d'})\, N_{d'}}\,(1 - a_{d,s}), \qquad CTS_d = \frac{R_{CTS}}{\sum_{d'} b_{d'}\, N_{d'}}\, b_d \tag{8.24}\]

where

  • \(PUI_{d,s}\) is public investment per person in decile \(d\) of sample \(s\), \(CTS_d\) current spending per person (local currency; codes pui, cts);
  • \(a_{d,s}\) is the share of the decile with access to the infrastructure PublicInfrastructureInvType (survey, percent / 100; code acs); \(1 - a\) is code noacc;
  • \(b_d\) is the ASPIRE benefit per person of the CurrentSpendingType programme in quintile \(\lceil d/2 \rceil\) (code aspbase; 0 if absent);
  • the sums run over the overall deciles; the amount per person without access (code puiamt) is then applied to each sample’s own access.

This is B1.6. Both are independent of the transfer settings and carry no median flag.

TipPseudocode: public investment and current spending 
rpui = rev * EXPCapex / 100
rcts = rev * EXPGoodsandserv / 100
for s in [ove, urb, rur]:
    noacc[s] = [1 - acs[s][d] / 100 for d in 1..10]       # access variable of the chosen type
puiamt = rpui / sum(noacc['ove'][d] * N[d] for d in 1..10)
for s in [ove, urb, rur]:
    pui[s] = [puiamt * noacc[s][d] for d in 1..10]
aspbase = [aspire.get(f'{iso}.{aspcode}{ceil(d / 2)}_preT_tot', 0) for d in 1..10]
ctsamt  = rcts / sum(aspbase[d] * N[d] for d in 1..10)
cts     = [ctsamt * aspbase[d] for d in 1..10]

8.9.4 Recycling gain in percent of consumption

Each amount per person is divided by the consumption per person of the sample’s decile:

\[ G_{d,s} = \frac{PIT_d + PUI_{d,s} + CTS_d + T_d}{c_{d,s}} \times 100 \tag{8.25}\]

where \(G_{d,s}\) is the recycling gain (percent of consumption; code rec.tot; its parts pit.tot, pui.tot, cts.tot, tgt.tot) and \(c_{d,s}\) consumption per person (Equation 8.12). Recycling rows have deciles only; the basket is blank.

8.10 Net effect, compensation and inequality

The net effect is what each decile gains or loses once the revenue comes back (section 5.4):

\[ net_{d,s} = e_{d,s} + G_{d,s} \tag{8.26}\]

where \(net_{d,s}\) is the net effect (percent of consumption; code net.tot). This is D1.6.

8.10.1 Compensation shares

How much of the revenue would it take to make each decile whole? The share of the total loss each decile bears:

\[ \chi_{d,s} = \frac{e_{d,s}\, X_{d,s}}{\sum_{d'} e_{d',s}\, X_{d',s}} \times 100 \tag{8.27}\]

where \(\chi_{d,s}\) is the compensation share of decile \(d\) (percent; code pctrev_comp; 0 when there is no loss). In each sample the shares sum to 100.

8.10.2 Gini coefficient

Inequality is measured three times: on consumption before the scenario, after the price effects, and after recycling.

\[ X^{x}_{d,s} = X_{d,s}\Big(1 + \frac{e_{d,s}}{100}\Big), \qquad X^{i}_{d,s} = X_{d,s}\Big(1 + \frac{net_{d,s}}{100}\Big) \tag{8.28}\]

\[ G(x) = 1 - \frac{\sum_{d=1}^{10} x_d\, W_d}{\sum_{d=1}^{10} x_d} \tag{8.29}\]

where

  • \(X^{x}\) and \(X^{i}\) are decile consumption after the price effects and after recycling (codes postx, posti);
  • \(G\) is the Gini coefficient of the decile totals \(x_d\); \(G_0 = G(X)\), \(G_x = G(X^x)\), \(G_i = G(X^i)\) (codes gini.g0, gini.gx, gini.gi);
  • \(W_d\) are fixed weights (DGiniW on DistMapping).

This is B1.7. The weights come from legacy’s Lorenz fit: a quadratic \(L(q) = a q^2 + b q\) through the origin fitted by least squares to the ten cumulative shares at \(q = 1, \dots, 10\), with \(G = (5 - \text{area under } L) / 5\). The fit is linear in the shares, so

\[ W_d = \frac{2}{n} \sum_{q \ge d} \omega_q, \qquad \omega_q = \frac{\tfrac{n^3}{3}(S_2 q^2 - S_3 q) + \tfrac{n^2}{2}(S_4 q - S_3 q^2)}{S_4 S_2 - S_3^2}, \qquad S_m = \sum_{q=1}^{n} q^m \tag{8.30}\]

with \(n = 10\). The weights are:

8.12 Price changes by production sector

The same input-output factors tell how much each producing sector’s costs rise. Section 11 lists the sectors hit hardest and summarises them by CPAT sector. The methodology treats pass-through in section 5.3.2.3 (eq-5_8).

\[ \bar{t}_s = \frac{\sum_{i \in s} Q_i\, t^{*}_i}{\sum_{i \in s} Q_i} \tag{8.32}\]

  • \(t^{*}_i\) is the output price change after pass-through (code dpsp.<i>);
